## Why it was obtained

A literature sweep reported that this paper prints closed forms for the curves
`docs/libbrecht-parameters.md` records as figure-only digitizations. The maker directed adoption
of its data set as the Phase 6 comparison target. Both claims are addressed below; **the first is
confirmed and is worth more than the second.**

## 1. The data set is 206 PHOTOGRAPHS, not a table — and its seed is a needle

> "This paper presents a matrix of 206 snow crystal growth observations as a function of
> temperature and water vapor supersaturation in air, each illustrating the morphology and size
> of a crystal forming on the tip of **an isolated c-axis ice needle** after a known growth time."

Stated uncertainties:

> "We estimate that the temperature uncertainties in these data are typically ±0.2 C, and that the
> water-vapor supersaturations are **between 0.8 and 1.2 times the stated values**."

Two consequences for using it as a Phase 6 target, both structural rather than incidental:

- **The observations live in Figure 2, a photographic matrix.** Growth times and image scales are
  printed per panel, but there is no numeric table of `(T, σ, habit)`. Turning it into a scoring
  target means digitizing 206 panels into morphology labels — a WP1-scale digitization with its
  own read uncertainty, on images whose 3-D structure Libbrecht himself says "takes some
  experience to understand… from the 2D image projections".
- **Every crystal grows on a c-axis needle tip.** Our registered seed is the 19-site hexagonal
  plate. The seed probe measured seed shape as the largest single systematic in Phase 6 (warm `AR`
  +57% from thickness alone), so scoring our plate-seeded runs against needle-seeded observations
  would be comparing different experiments. Matching it requires the column-seed change the plan
  already flags as ADR-level and which gates 111 of 122 held-out entries.

Libbrecht's own framing of the bar:

> "At present, it is not clear that any existing 3D computational models of snow crystal growth
> can adequately reproduce **any** of the structures seen in Figure 2 under prescribed growth
> conditions, let alone reproducing the entire set with even modest fidelity."

## 2. It prints CLOSED FORMS for σ₀(T) — including a no-SDAK variant

This is the find. `α(T, σ_surf) = exp(−σ₀/σ_surf)` (Eq. 6), with **`A = 1` for every facet**:

> "To keep M1 relatively simple, we chose to set `A = 1` in Equation 3 for all growth conditions"

**M1 — with the SDAK dips**, σ₀ in **percent**, `T` the magnitude in °C:

```
σ₀,basal(T) = (0.02·T^1.75 + 0.3) · (1 − 0.87·exp(−(log T − log 4.5)² / 0.07))
σ₀,prism(T) = (0.015·T²    + 0.02·T^0.6) · (1 − 0.95·exp(−(log T − log 14.4)² / 0.06))
```

**M2 — broad facets, the SAME curves WITHOUT the dips.** This is exactly our no-SDAK case:

```
σ₀,basal(T) = 0.02·T^1.75 + 0.3
σ₀,prism(T) = 0.015·T²    + 0.02·T^0.6
```

> "While these functional forms are completely ad hoc, the values of σ₀(T) derive mainly from
> experimental data."

## 3. What the closed forms say about our digitized curves, and about SDAK

Computed, not asserted (`scratchpad/m2-vs-digitized.mjs`):

**M2 against our digitized anchors** — ratio closed-form / ours:

| T (°C) | −2 | −3 | −5 | −8 | −10 | −15 | −20 | −30 | −40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| basal | 1.05 | 0.97 | 0.91 | 0.95 | 1.02 | 1.08 | 1.07 | 1.14 | 1.17 |
| prism | **3.23** | **2.48** | **1.58** | 1.25 | 1.13 | 1.09 | 1.02 | 1.05 | 1.12 |

**Basal agrees within ~17% everywhere.** Prism agrees from −8 °C colder — and **diverges by a
factor of 1.6 to 3.2 at the warm end**, far outside the ±25% digitization band those anchors
carry. The independent review's caution that the warm-side prism anchors sit one-sidedly low is
confirmed, and it is worse than the review estimated: not 25–40% low, but low by a factor of two
to three at −2…−5 °C. **Every warm-end result this project has produced rests on a σ₀_prism that
Libbrecht's own printed form puts 2–3× higher.**

**Crossing counts — the structural result:**

| curves | crossings of σ₀,basal = σ₀,prism |
|---|---|
| **M2 (no SDAK)** | **one**, at −8.39 °C |
| ours (digitized, no SDAK) | **one**, at −10.00 °C |
| **M1 (with SDAK dips)** | **five**: −3.70, −6.25, **−8.46**, **−9.93**, −18.62 °C |

**This is an independent, quantitative confirmation of ADR 0005's provenance analysis, computed
from Libbrecht's own published functions.** The no-SDAK curves cross exactly once, however they
are parameterised — ours at −10.00 °C, his at −8.39 °C. Adding the SDAK dips turns one crossing
into **five**, and they land at −3.70 and −9.93 °C, against Nakaya's digitized boundaries at
−3.3 and −9.9 °C.

ADR 0005 said the SDAK dip locations "were chosen to impose agreement with Nakaya" and therefore
that a SDAK model reproducing the diagram is an in-sample result. That was an argument from the
source's prose. It is now a measurement: **the dips are what create the extra crossings, and the
extra crossings sit on the reference's boundaries.**

It also settles the no-SDAK structural claim quantitatively. "One monotone crossing cannot produce
three transitions" holds for *both* parameterisations of the broad-facet curves, so it is a
property of the physics rather than of our digitization.

## 4. The Kelly & Boyer correction (N3) — confirmed here, verbatim

> "[Brief technical aside: In the CA model, it is not necessary to 'invert' the function α(σ) as
> was suggested in [2014Kel]. Rather one can numerically relax the external supersaturation field
> while **simultaneously** relaxing the boundary values of σ and α(σ) at each point on the ice
> surface. In this way, only the direct function α(σ) is needed]."

The stretch register's attribution of this correction to 2306.13087 was recorded as unconfirmed;
it is now confirmed. `solver-cpu/src/lk-solver.ts` conforms on both counts — checked 2026-07-27
and recorded in the Phase 6 plan.

## 5. SDAK's own status, in the source's words

> "the SDAK phenomenon provides the only viable option currently available that can adequately
> explain the Nakaya diagram together with a plethora of other ice-growth data."

> "We still treat the SDAK phenomenon as a working hypothesis."

This is the framing guard for any Phase 6 report: a no-SDAK model failing to reproduce the diagram
is **Libbrecht's own published expectation**, not a discovery of ours. Phase 6 is the first
independent *test* of it, never its discovery.
