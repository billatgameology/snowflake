# Educational website design guide

A reusable working standard for educational websites that combine explanation, visuals,
narration and interaction. Its purpose is to help a first-time learner understand a relationship,
explain it in ordinary words and use it to make a prediction. It accumulates lessons from iterative
production and review; it is not evidence that a particular treatment works for every audience.

This document stands alone. Keep subject-specific examples, individual treatments, feedback
quotations, acceptance records, asset names, voice choices, implementation commands and release
status in the adopting project's own brief, review records and runbook. Copying this guide does
not carry production permissions, purchases, language commitments or publishing authority with it.

## How to apply this guide

Start with a small project brief: intended learners, their likely prior knowledge, the central
question, supported languages, available evidence, device constraints and how understanding will
be checked. Decide which media and interactions serve that question. A narrated animation is an
option, not a requirement for every explanation.

Use the existing outline, script and review record; no additional registry is needed.

1. Choose one question and a plain-language takeaway. Trace its prerequisites to demonstrations
   the learner can actually encounter.
2. Draft words and visible explanations together. Inspect available assets before choosing to
   reuse, adapt or create a treatment. Choose by teaching purpose, not availability alone.
3. Use the teaching-beat worksheet below to specify the subject, changed input, visible action,
   result, likely misconception and connection to the next idea.
4. Check the claims and what the images imply against the evidence. Prototype the hardest
   explanation with provisional narration or an equivalent reading walkthrough.
5. Review the complete experience at its intended pace before multiplying the treatment or
   committing to expensive production. Record partial inspection as partial.
6. Produce the authorized media, align it with the explanation, and integrate the actual
   learner controls, reading alternatives and delivery paths.
7. Review the resulting experience, repair specific failures and recheck their surrounding
   transitions. Report technical checks, editorial judgment and learner understanding separately.

The amount of content and its duration should follow the investigation. Preserve useful prior work
and specifically approved features while addressing the identified problem.

## Build usable understanding

**Demonstrate prerequisites before depending on them.** Establish a concrete subject, show what
happens, explain the relationship and let the learner use it. A definition or an earlier mention
alone does not establish understanding. When a learner gets lost, trace backward to the first
missing explanation rather than only rewriting the passage where confusion becomes visible.

**Separate event, explanation, measurement and calculation.** Let each have a clear purpose.
Introduce a technical term when its referent has acquired ordinary-language meaning. Explain any
necessary symbol, axis, unit, colour or mark before asking the learner to reason with it.

**Budget novelty.** Prefer one new kind of demand in a teaching beat: the idea, its terminology or
its visual representation. Introduce a new representation through a known relationship; explain a
new relationship with familiar visual conventions. Assemble the overview after its parts make sense.
This is a sequencing aid, not a quota on words, drawings or cuts.

**Keep depth accessible.** Retain the causal steps, essential conditions and important limits in
the main explanation. Place optional derivations, historical context and additional examples in a
substantive reader when they interrupt the central question. That reader must contain the actual
explanation and precise sources, not just a bibliography. Track omitted material as taught here,
provided elsewhere or deliberately deferred with a reason.

**Make every detour earn its return.** Ask what the preceding demonstration allows the learner to
understand next. A correct and interesting method can still distract if its result is never used.
Move that depth to optional reading when appropriate. A transition sentence cannot repair two
unconnected investigations.

**Test use, not agreement.** Ask an uncoached learner what happened, why, what would change under a
new condition and what the explanation does not establish. Record the answer and first missing
link. Familiarity with terminology, agreement with the narrator and a satisfaction score do not
by themselves demonstrate understanding.

## Write as a helpful narrator

Start with something the learner can notice. Use concrete subjects, ordinary verbs and natural
sentences. An honest question should lead to something observable. A useful pattern is
notice → question → follow the cause → name the idea → use it; vary it when the explanation needs
another rhythm.

Accessible language assumes little specialist knowledge while preserving the mechanism and its
conditions. Keep qualifications in natural language. Avoid unexplained compound terms, abstract
lesson announcements, repeated reassurance, claims that something is obvious and language that
gives nonintentional processes a purpose.

Questions need not turn every passage into a quiz. Keep production instructions out of spoken
copy. Introduce a cited person or method through its relevant contribution before relying on its
name as authority. Prefer useful context over biography or chronology that delays the argument.

## Explanation and visual design

### Establish identity and attention

A first-time learner should be able to identify the key subject, its state and its scale. Use
shape, texture, direct labels or a stable legend as needed; colour alone is insufficient. Explain
what a representative mark stands for and what it must not be taken to mean.

Select the exact referent of words such as “this,” “here” or “one” before the important action.
Point, highlight, isolate or enlarge while retaining enough context to locate it. Return to an
overview when comparison benefits from it.

For each beat, decide what enters now, what remains as a useful reference and what can retire.
Do not reveal all the results at entry and merely highlight them later. An overview can orient
the learner without displaying every answer or annotation in advance.

### Make the explanatory action visible

Treat a narrated verb as a visual obligation. If the explanation says something arrives, changes,
accumulates, reverses or disappears, the relevant visible state must do so at that passage's pace.
Show the changed input as well as the response. A different caption over an identical picture
cannot demonstrate a changed condition.

Use subject → input → action → result as a starting pattern. Hold the result long enough for
inspection. Fix timing, framing and contrast before adding decorative movement. A quiet passage
can be useful when the learner knows what to inspect; a long hold on an unexplained image is not
an explanation.

Motion should reveal a mechanism, route or consequence. A path must reach its intended endpoint;
labels, directions and visible changes must agree. Where opposing processes and their net result
both matter, distinguish them. A camera movement does not demonstrate a change in the subject.

There is no minimum number of animations. A single evolving image, a diagram, an actual recording
or an interactive comparison may serve the explanation. Choose the representation that makes the
relationship legible, and identify whether it is observation, reconstruction, illustration or model
output. Attractive rendering does not establish validity.

### Preserve continuity and accumulated history

Carry an identifiable subject through connected ideas. Introduce a close-up through a selection or
linked parent view. Make a change of scale, viewpoint, specimen, example or representation explicit.
If spatial separation is exaggerated for inspection, identify that transformation.

Retain a useful invariant across transitions: position, framing, scale, selected part or surrounding
conditions. Then change the intended variable. Close a comparison back to its continuing subject
before starting a new investigation.

Preserve irreversible changes and accumulated history: added parts, depleted quantities, distances,
counts and earlier measurements. A new section is not automatically a new starting state. Identify
a rewind or separate run explicitly, and retain the first result when it supports the next comparison.
Do not change meaningful coordinates merely to make the drawing fit.

### Make comparisons interpretable

State what changes and what stays fixed, then make those controls visible. Use a before-outline,
starting mark, common scale or matched view. Keep camera framing, marker units and spatial scale
consistent where the comparison depends on them. Correct words cannot compensate for misleading
visual proportions.

If equal elapsed time is part of the evidence, show both processes over the matched interval with
readable time references. Keep the starting marks and results afterward. A caption beside two
completed outcomes cannot, by itself, demonstrate their evolution over that interval.

Establish the quantity and purpose before displaying numbers. Explain units, denominators and
reference conditions. Show absolute amounts when percentages could mislead; common units and
consistent rounding must support the visible comparison. Invented teaching counters must be
identified as illustrative and kept distinct from measured quantities.

### Show how evidence becomes a conclusion

Begin with the observation in a viewpoint that makes it readable. Allow the learner to notice the
relevant pattern before placing an inferred or calculated representation beside it. Do not animate
an unobserved event as if the source had recorded it, or imply exact correspondence between unrelated
specimens, images or datasets.

For measurement, perform the chain that matters to the argument:

**controlled subject → change → signal or observable reading → interval or reference → inferred quantity.**

Identify the instrument's input and output. Where calibration matters, show the setting being
adjusted and the response reaching the reference condition; a badge declaring calibration is not
a demonstration. If several signals contribute to a readout, show how they meet or combine before
interpreting the output.

Keep the observation visible while showing what a prediction misses and what an improved explanation
accounts for. Distinguish the measured data, assumptions and fitted quantities. Never invent
measurements for a convenient story. A better fit can support a comparison without proving a unique
mechanism or a wider claim than the evidence allows.

### Close the argument

Revisit the causal steps in a short visible sequence, carrying the learner through what changed
and why. A spoken list over an already-completed picture rarely does the same work. End by showing
what has been answered and what remains unresolved. A follow-on question should emerge from that
result, and the linked material must actually address it.

## Visual hierarchy and access

Give the explanatory subject enough space and contrast. Labels should identify an otherwise
ambiguous object, quantity, condition or change. Introduce them when useful and remove redundant
ones rather than shrinking them into illegibility. Avoid stacking a navigation title, subheading,
action heading and full-sentence restatement over the same picture.

Keep primary playback and reading controls easy to find. Secondary navigation and extended source
notes can live in accessible secondary surfaces. Keep inference-critical conditions next to the
claim they qualify; optional detail is no substitute for a condition needed to interpret the image.
Any visible model or source indicator must describe the representation actually on screen.

Compose for narrow screens as well as large ones. Maintain readable on-screen text after canvas
scaling, clear moving boundaries and space for controls. Check intermediate poses and complete
compositions, not just individual labels or endpoints. Add a stable group identity when overlapping
parts must be distinguished without overwriting existing colour meanings.

Provide useful text, captions and static or reduced-motion alternatives. Preserve the current
idea, before-reference and result without exposing a later answer too soon. A static mode should
be an authored explanatory state, not an arbitrary frozen frame. Motion and colour must not be
the sole carriers of meaning. Essential interactions need keyboard and touch access as well as
pointer access; hover alone is insufficient.

## Narration and timing contract

Words, images and pauses form one performance. A correct transcript beside a correct diagram can
still fail if the action occurs before the learner knows where to look.

- Review exact spoken copy and diagram implications before recording. Keep speech, optional
  reading and production notes separate. Use the voices, languages and production scope authorized
  by the project; the guide itself supplies none of those decisions.
- When spoken words change, update the affected authorized recording and its captions, alignment
  and visual cues together. Visual-only edits need no new speech if the recording remains accurate.
  Reuse unchanged takes when text, voice, settings and recorded bytes match the intended revision.
- Let actual media time drive captions, visuals and guided movement. Buffering or failed playback
  must not leave an independent visual clock advancing. Provisional rehearsal timing stays marked
  provisional until it is aligned with the delivered performance.
- Use measured phrase timing for important reveals. Missing or ambiguous cue matches should be
  resolved explicitly, not silently guessed. Label interpolated timing as such; paragraph endpoints
  do not establish word-level precision.
- Pace difficult passages locally. Allow time to identify the subject, see the action and inspect
  the result. A speaking-rate number can flag a concern but cannot certify clarity. Rehearse after
  timing edits; do not apply one successful pause length or tempo everywhere.
- For a prediction, show the changed input before posing the question and withhold result motion
  and answer labels until the release. Make an editorial hold distinguishable from a real steady
  state. Preserve any ongoing process needed to understand the question.
- After joins, pauses or tempo edits, derive the timeline from the delivered decoded audio and
  recheck cues and transitions at ordinary speed. Keep original takes separate from derivatives.
- Preserve source identity, request text, non-secret settings, alignment and delivered-file identity.
  Do not overwrite earlier masters, log credentials or automatically retry an uncertain paid request.
  Investigate its outcome first so completed work is not charged or produced twice.
- Check the actual delivered media for source coverage, joins, duration, loudness and clipping;
  verify that the site serves that file. Derive integrity checks from the bytes rather than only a
  producer's success report. Identify synthetic narration where used. Human listening remains
  necessary to assess pronunciation, delivery and emotional fit; alignment is not listening.

### Multiple languages

Preserve meaning, qualification and representational cues across supported languages, including
diagram labels and controls. A missing translation that silently falls back can hide incomplete work;
check required coverage explicitly. Do not pair an outdated performance with a rewritten explanation.
If a language is deferred, provide an explicit fallback or availability state and preserve prior assets.

When switching languages, preserve the learner's conceptual location through reviewed semantic
anchors, not the elapsed seconds or a fraction of total duration. The selected recording owns its
clock and displayed duration. Preserve meaningful holds and answer-release states across different
speaking rhythms.

Stop the previous recording before starting the new one. Retain speed, paused state and manual
reading mode; invalidate stale asynchronous play requests and apply the latest desired position
when media becomes ready. Test rapid changes, round trips, buffering, errors and switches during a
prediction hold. A paused language change must not start playback unexpectedly. Fluent listening
and translation review remain separate from machine timing checks.

## Interaction and delivery

**Keep the learner in control.** Media starts through an intentional action. Manual reading or
scrolling can take over from guided movement, with a clear way to resume. Distinguish the site's
own delayed scroll events from real user input. A touch on explanatory artwork should still allow
page scrolling unless the learner has deliberately entered a manipulation control.

**Use consistent state.** Playback, seeking, reading and reduced motion should reach corresponding
explanatory states. Forward and reverse seeking must reconstruct a coherent current state rather
than depend on having played every earlier frame. Coordinate media ownership so hidden content
cannot continue speaking or take control unexpectedly.

**Make asynchronous transitions reliable.** Preserve a Play request made before content loads.
Cancel obsolete work when the learner changes content, exits or replays; a stale renderer or play
promise must not change or dispose the replacement. Test replay, resizing, loading failure and
recovery as well as first entry.

**Keep navigation predictable.** Support direct entry and onward links as well as the main entry
path. Secondary controls must not unexpectedly move reading geometry or restart playback. Provide
clear focus, keyboard dismissal and focus return. Preserve intentional pause and accessibility
preferences through navigation.

**Load and render within a budget.** Load substantial content when needed and prefetch deliberately.
Keep the initial experience independent of the entire catalog. Limit simultaneous rendering resources,
release hidden work and handle context loss with a legible state. Use capability detection and treat
common fallback paths as designed experiences. Compare their explanatory quality, not just whether
they start.

**Verify on the supported devices.** Check narrow layouts, touch scrolling, control sizes, safe-area
clearance, memory pressure and media-start restrictions. Choose explicit targets for the project.
An emulated phone viewport checks composition and some interactions; it does not prove behavior on
a physical device or another browser engine.

**Deliver the reviewed artifact.** Verify served text, links, media and reachable source information.
Keep internal production language out of visitor-facing copy. Make incomplete or intentionally
unreleased material an explicit state, and verify that public routes and assets honor it. Deployment
commands, release switches and environment setup belong in the project's runbook.

## Teaching-beat worksheet

Use this within an existing script or storyboard. Group sentences that share one explanatory action;
a beat need not correspond to a page, cut or fixed duration.

| Field | What to supply |
| --- | --- |
| Learning purpose | Question, takeaway and something the learner should be able to explain or predict. |
| Prerequisites and depth | Earlier demonstrations that earn this step; optional material and where it is available. |
| Evidence and limits | Source, conditions, representation type and likely misleading inference to prevent. |
| Words and timing | Spoken or written cue; provisional versus measured timing; inspection or prediction hold. |
| Subject and focus | Identifiable referent, state, scale, selection and any linked parent view. |
| Visible explanation | Changed input → action → result, with a fixed reference and a clear account of what the motion means. |
| Build order | What is new, what enters now, what remains for comparison and what can retire. |
| Continuity | What survives the incoming and outgoing transition; any explicit reset, new example or scale change. |
| Access and interaction | Narrow composition, text/static alternative, controls and resource constraints. |
| Review | Artifact identity, observed failure, repair, actual checks and remaining uncertainty. |

For comparisons, include common units, reference quantities, held-fixed conditions and time. For
measurements, include the observable signal and how it supports the inferred quantity. For quiet
passages, name what the learner is inspecting.

## Review the experience

For a first-impression editorial review, watch or read at the intended pace before studying the
implementation, without scrubbing through a narrated performance. This reveals what the experience
actually communicates. If the reviewer already knows the implementation, state that context rather
than calling the review a fresh learner test. A source-only review is useful but has a different scope.

Ask in learner language:

- What am I looking at, and what does this mark represent?
- What changed while those words were spoken? Could I notice it without replay?
- What stayed fixed, and how does the comparison support the claim?
- Do these numbers now mean something, or do I only see that they differ?
- Is this the same subject at another scale, or did the example change?
- What does this passage let me understand next?
- Was an answer visible before I had a chance to reason about it?

Record a finding as **location and cue → what the learner actually encounters → likely wrong
inference → smallest useful repair**. Diagnose missing meaning, identity, timing or causal action
before asking for more detail or animation. Treat a contradiction, unidentified subject or invisible
key change as a repair; distinguish those failures from optional taste suggestions.

Then replay the suspect passage with its incoming and outgoing transitions. Inspect intermediate
states, narrow layouts, reverse seeking and static alternatives. If a shared drawing or control
changes, check all uses, including recaps whose words did not change. Text assertions and geometry
bounds do not prove that the visible composition is readable.

Review a current artifact: rebuild or reload as needed after changes, confirm its source/media
identity, and observe a fresh error window. After a seek or selection, verify the requested position
and the current visual instance have settled before judging readiness; a previous instance's ready
indicator is not evidence about its replacement.

Use a bounded non-author review for important claims and explanatory passages. Record who reviewed,
whether context was shared, which artifacts and passages they inspected and what was not checked.
A successful automatic playthrough is not continuous human viewing. Sequential screenshots are
sampled inspection. Neither demonstrates listening quality or uncoached learner comprehension.

After the identified repair and its necessary regression checks, stop unless a new finding warrants
more work. Choose checks for the changed failure surface; a prose correction does not require an
unrelated simulation suite. Do not replace explanation work with an expanding review bureaucracy.

## Completion checklist and acceptance boundaries

Use the existing review record and mark each applicable item checked, needs repair or not checked.

| Area | Observable check |
| --- | --- |
| Content | Prerequisites are demonstrated; language and images preserve the claim's conditions; optional depth is available. |
| Visual explanation | Input, action, reference and result are legible at the intended pace; transitions preserve identity and history. |
| Narration | Delivered words and media match; important cues and holds align; technical audio checks and actual listening are reported separately. |
| Access and interaction | Reading, captions, static/reduced-motion states, seeking, manual takeover, navigation and supported input methods work. |
| Delivery | Direct and linked entries work; served assets match the reviewed revision; loading, errors, fallback and release states were exercised. |
| Devices | Actual viewport, browser and device coverage is named; emulation is distinguished from physical-device testing. |
| Editorial review | Important passages received a non-author review with its scope, findings and fixes recorded. |
| Understanding | An uncoached learner's explanation and prediction are recorded, or this remains explicitly not checked. |

Keep technical readiness, editorial judgment, creator feedback, human listening and learner
understanding separate. Praise applies to the feature actually named. Passing tests, a successful
deployment or the absence of another complaint does not establish the other forms of acceptance.
No average score should hide a missing causal explanation.

## Keep the guide learning without making it project-specific

After a meaningful iteration:

1. Record the exact feedback, affected content, repair and verification in the project's review record.
2. Identify the transferable failure: what could recur with different subject matter, assets and tools?
3. Check whether an existing principle already covers it. If so, improve its application; do not add
   a duplicate rule because it was missed once.
4. When a gap remains, refine the relevant principle with its purpose, observable check and limits.
   Describe what should be understandable, not the particular graphic or implementation that fixed it.
5. Remove or revise contradicted guidance. Preserve the earlier quotation and response in project
   history without keeping obsolete directions active here.
6. Read the amendment as someone starting an unrelated educational website. It should make sense
   without the original subject, source files, production history or software stack.

Keep a compact working standard, not a growing chronology. A successful treatment is evidence to
consider, not a universal template. Each new application still needs review with its own learners,
content and delivery constraints.
