Thirty concepts developed to hook-and-beats level but not scripted. Ordered by how ready
they are. All trace to education chapters 1–13; every one carries the chapter it rests on.

Full concept detail (beats, visual spine, sourcing, risks) for the ch 9–13 batch is archived
at `scratchpad/concepts-ch9-13.md`; the ch 1–4 and ch 5–8 batches live in the session record.

## The three gaps to fill first

The editorial review found these missing from the slate as a whole. They matter more than
any individual concept below.

### 1. A failure video — the missing Mark Rober engine

All twelve launch scripts are finished explanations delivered with confidence. The
uncertainty on display is always *the field's*, never *ours*. Rober's actual engine is a
build with the failures shown.

**The material exists.** The strongest candidate: a render whose sixfold symmetry silently
broke because of floating-point rounding — invisible at one setting, obvious at a larger
step. That is a 45-second story with a real reveal, and it demonstrates the channel's
honesty far more convincingly than any correctly-hedged prose about somebody else's
uncertainty. **Right now the honesty is a policy. This video would make it a behaviour.**

*Note: this draws on chapters 14–29 (the build arc), which is otherwise out of scope. It is
the one crossover worth making early.*

### 2. A 20–30 second piece

Every launch script is 45–70 s and the average is ~60. That uniform rhythm suppresses reach.
One short piece is already sitting inside script 02: **"Six arms leave at the same instant,
and nothing coordinated them — they just shared a past."** Complete in 25 seconds.

### 3. A near-silent spectacle piece

Nothing on the slate is mostly-image. The **seaweed split-screen** — same instability,
directional preference switched off, completely different object — is arresting enough to
carry a whole video and currently gets 4.5 buried seconds inside script 06. Ten words of
narration, thirty seconds, let the render work. Cheapest reach on the board, and it reaches
people who would never click a snowflake explainer.

## Concepts deliberately rejected

- **"Why six sides" as a straight explainer** — covered by script 04 from an angle that
  doesn't announce itself as the obvious video.
- **"No two alike" as a straight explainer** — script 09 does it properly, with the word
  that makes it true.
- **Any temperature-to-shape causal story beyond what ch 7 supports.** The single most
  tempting wrong caption available, and it will suggest itself repeatedly.
- **Anything from `LibbrechtKinetics` or Phase 6.** Active and incomplete.
- **Anything embedding a source figure.** Rights-blocked, permanently.
