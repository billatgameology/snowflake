## Caption

The wetter the cloud, the branchier the crystal — and it's still starving. It eats faster than the air can resupply, so any bump that pokes out reaches richer air and runs away with it.

3. Flat Is A Valid Answer. It's Just Not A Stable One.

## Production notes

**Exists:** the 701-frame timeline with vapour field toggleable; cutaway views; freeze and scrub.

**Must be built:** (a) the perimeter depth readout tracing halo depth around the crystal — derived from the existing field dump, but the one genuinely new instrument here; (b) the three-panel bump diagram — the script's required 2D beat and its spine, give it the most animation budget; (c) the pencil beat; (d) **the isotropic companion run** — a second 701-frame timeline with directional preference dialled off, producing the seaweed form. Needs a solver run and a render pass.

**Fallback if (d) slips:** replace the seaweed panel with a 2D DIAGRAM of the same contrast. Do not substitute a still.

**Difficulty:** high — the only launch script needing new solver output.

**Hard note:** the halo readout is a model measurement and must never be narrated as proof. The VO says "look at the shortage," not "here's the evidence." The mechanism — shielding on both sides — is what carries the claim, and it is sourced independently.

**VAPOUR-FIELD LEGEND — slate-wide convention.** Scripts 06, 07 and 08 share this asset and an earlier draft gave them opposite colour meanings. The convention for the whole channel, matching chapter 4's animation ("the stronger the blue tint, the emptier the air"):

> **strong blue = emptied · pale/neutral = full supply**

The legend appears on **every frame** that shows the field. Do not invert it for aesthetics on any single video.

**Under-sold asset (editor's note, adopted):** the seaweed split-screen at 50.0 s is one of the most arresting images in the whole project and currently gets 4.5 buried seconds. It is strong enough to carry its own near-silent 30 s piece — see the reserve bench.
