## Caption

You can't photograph humidity, so he photographed the hole in it: a droplet fog that stops at one exact value. Then switched the fog off — and the crystal kept growing. All visuals RECREATION.

## Production notes

**Exists:** the vapour-field animation with the depletion shell locked to the outline (3.6 s) is the chapter-4 "Watch the halo form" asset and can be captured directly. Cell-true and cutaway views cover the plate-on-pedestal beat.

**Must be built:** plate-on-pedestal cutaway with the top-down swing (~1 day; it must read clearly that the plate hangs *free* above the glass or the moat beat makes no sense); the droplet-fog stipple with clean moat **and** its evaporation sweep (~2 days — **these two shots must be the same animation at two settings, or the control is visually a cheat; do not build them separately**); contour overlay snapping one line onto the moat edge; circle-vs-six-lobes strike-through.

**Difficulty:** highest on the launch slate. The video *is* a proof, so the visuals carry the argument rather than decorating it.

**RIGHTS — hard blocker:** the source photographs for this entire video (Figs. 3.3, 9.16, 9.17) are Libbrecht's and may never be shown, reproduced, traced, or rotoscoped. Every frame is our own animation. The **RECREATION** strap appears from 1.6 s and never leaves — every frame, not once at the top. The chapter-4 laboratory time-lapse (SnowCrystals.com) is copyright and offline-research-only; it must not appear in any published cut.

**Language gate:** no Greek character in any voiceover line or on-screen overlay. The threshold is always "the droplet threshold" or "the droplet-condensation contour". Check burned-in captions too — that is where a symbol most easily leaks back in.
