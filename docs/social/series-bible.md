# Series bible

Everything a producer needs to make more of this without asking. Built 2026-08-05 from a
four-agent concept sweep over education chapters 1–13, an adversarial fact-check, and a
short-form editorial review.

---

## The promise

> **"It's this channel about snowflakes where the real answer is always the opposite of what you'd think."**

That is the sentence a viewer repeats to a friend. It was chosen over two alternatives:

- *"Every answer comes stamped with how sure we actually are."* — this is the channel's
  **identity**, but it isn't something a sixteen-year-old says out loud. It's what they
  discover around video eight and then can't unsee. Correct place for it.
- *"Some guy is growing a snowflake inside a computer…"* — ties the ceiling to a build that
  is incomplete and out of chapter 1–13 scope. **Hold in reserve as the Series 2 promise**,
  where chapters 14–29 make it true.

The inversion is not packaging. It is what the source material actually says, at every
scale, which means the format generator never runs out and never has to cheat to stay
interesting. The honesty payload rides *inside* it: "and here's the part that's also
backwards — nobody actually knows" is the same structural move as every other episode, not a
genre break.

## The seven formats

| Format | Structure | Capacity | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **BACKWARDS** | 0–2 s spoken inversion, no setup · state the belief in the viewer's voice · show the wrong picture, then kill it · mechanism in one 2D beat + one render beat · land on the object on their sleeve | ~25–30 | **MED** — one bespoke 2D animation each time. The channel's bottleneck. |
| **GROW IT** | A question about the *finished* shape · "so let's watch it happen" · play to the instant, **freeze** · scrub back 20 frames, replay at quarter speed · zoom out, land the answer, loop to the seed | ~12–15 off existing runs | **LOW per episode / HIGH per run** (~12 h replay). Best cost curve on the channel — keep a run always baking. |
| **HOW SURE ARE WE** | The question, flat · the answer, delivered with total confidence · **the turn at ~20 s: "okay — how good is that answer, actually?"** · the four-kinds-of-line graphic · what's doing the work vs the guessing · the badge | ~30 | **LOW–MED** — the recurring graphic amortises to near zero after episode three. |
| **THE PROOF** | An impossible measurement task · "so you don't. Here's what he did instead." · the trick, as an apparatus diagram assembling on screen · the number it produced · what it cost / what broke | ~10–12 | **LOW–MED** — 2D only. Uses no render time, which is why it keeps the schedule alive while runs bake. |
| **THE OBSESSIVE** | The absurd commitment as a flat fact · what they actually did, inconvenience left in · what it bought science · what it cost | ~7–9 | **LOW** — PD archive plus voice. Cheapest good pieces available, and the channel's only parasocial anchor given no face on camera. |
| **CUT IT OPEN** | A crystal that looks solid · "there's something inside this" · the cut, slow, one continuous move · the inside contradicts the outside · why it had to be that way | ~6–8 | **LOW–MED** |
| **RETRACTION** | "I got this wrong." · what was said, replayed · what's actually true and how it was caught · what that means for the rest | demand-driven | **LOW** |

**RETRACTION is a standing commitment, not a slot.** Zero uses is a fine number.
**Manufacturing a mistake to run this format is the most tempting and most disqualifying
tactic available to this channel.** If it is ever staged, everything else the channel says
about confidence becomes theatre.

### Two hard format rules

**GROW IT:** the crystals are a Gravner–Griffeath cellular automaton, not physics. The
timeline may only *illustrate* a fact the corpus independently establishes. It may never be
the evidence for a claim. **If the only support for a statement is that the simulation did
it, the statement does not get made.**

**BACKWARDS:** the inversion is legitimate only if **the corpus itself states the
counterintuitive form**. If the writer had to invent the "you'd think", it is not a
BACKWARDS video and the fact gets told straight in another format. The corpus supplies more
genuine inversions than this channel can publish in a year; there is no reason to invent
one — which is exactly why inventing one would mean something has already gone wrong
upstream.

| 9 | **10** Flat Tall Flat Tall | HOW SURE (tier 4) | First NOBODY KNOWS. Needs the badge system to already mean something. |

**One honesty conflict this creates, and its fix.** Script 02 was designed as the first place
the model disclosure is *spoken aloud*, and it now sits at position 4. Three videos would
ship with only the corner tag. The tag discipline technically holds, but "spoken aloud" is
the stronger commitment and should not be quietly dropped for retention.

**Fix:** put a three-second spoken disclosure into whatever ships first — *"these are
renders; a computer model, not photographs"* — and let 02 be the video that explains it at
length. Commitment kept, placement kept.

## Cadence

**Three per week, fixed days.** Not daily. The bespoke 2D animation in BACKWARDS is the
bottleneck, and the top failure mode is quality collapse, not obscurity. Three a week
against a ~100–130 piece corpus is roughly eight months of runway — long enough that the
schedule never forces a bad decision.

| Slot | Content |
|---|---|
| 1 | The engine — BACKWARDS |
| 2 | A cheap format — THE PROOF or THE OBSESSIVE |
| 3 | Asset-forward — GROW IT or CUT IT OPEN |

- **One in five published pieces is HOW SURE ARE WE**, displacing whichever slot it lands in.
  It needs to feel like furniture, not like a special episode.
- **Never two of the same format back to back.** Not even two strong ones.
- **Tier-4 budget: one per month, hard cap.** There are exactly seven tier-4 questions in
  the bank. Never two in a row; never used to rescue a slow week. **The ratio of SETTLED
  endings to NOBODY-KNOWS endings never drops below 3:1** — below that, the channel's
  message silently inverts from "here's how we know things" to "scientists don't know
  anything."
- **Tag by mechanism, not by hook.** Hook-bank items 4, 5, 7, 9 and 12 are the same physical
  idea at four scales. **No two pieces sharing a mechanism inside a 10-video window.**
- **Cap BACKWARDS at 40% of published pieces**, and vary delivery inside the cap.
- **Never let publishing wait on a render.** Keep the queue continuously occupied. The cheap
  formats exist structurally to absorb render delays — that is half their job.
- **Seasonal weighting.** Front-load the strongest and most searchable pieces into
  November–February. *"It's too cold to snow"* is held deliberately for the first cold snap
  rather than burned in August.
- **An empty slot is cheaper than a correction.** Never publish an UNSOURCED claim to fill a
  gap.

## Retention mechanics

The specific problem: the finished object is **beautiful, static, and utterly familiar**.
Every viewer has seen a six-pointed star ten thousand times and it now reads as stock
imagery. Everything below follows from that one fact.

### Past 3 seconds

- **Never open on a finished symmetric crystal.** It signals "screensaver." Open on
  something the viewer cannot immediately classify: the cell-true blocky view, a
  cross-section, a *triangular* one, a hollow column end-on, or a mid-growth frame that
  looks broken.
- **Open on motion the subject supposedly doesn't have.** Frame 1 of the timeline, already
  moving before the first word. Growth is the only motion a snow crystal has and almost no
  viewer has seen it. Spend it in the first second; don't save it.
- **The spoken hook lands before 2.0 s.** No branding, no greeting.
- **Prefer negation openers.** "It was never liquid." "It's got nothing to do with cold."
  "Nothing chose the hexagon." These attack a belief the viewer is holding *at that instant*
  — a different and stronger mechanism than curiosity.

### Past 10 seconds

- **The scrub-back.** Play forward, freeze on the instant, reverse twenty frames, replay at
  quarter speed while narrating. Rewind is a retention primitive and the timeline gives it
  free. Make it the channel's signature move.
- **Scale ratchets at 8–12 s.** One number as the *second* payload, after the hook lands and
  before the mechanism starts: a hundred thousand droplets; the seed at one part in a
  hundred thousand; a strip of ice 200 molecules wide; a 1%-covered floor dropping the
  humidity twentyfold; 318 years from Kepler's question to the answer.
- **Look-switching as an edit beat.** Cutting from `footage-ice` to `ggview` mid-video is a
  hard visual change that resets attention *and* does epistemic work — beauty, then the
  model's actual cells. Costs nothing; the looks are already locked.
- **Nothing sits still.** Slow rotation or dolly under every talking passage. A static hero
  render reads as a screensaver in about 1.5 seconds.

### To the end

- **Answer at ~60% of runtime, then turn.** A video whose answer arrives at the end has
  taught the viewer they can leave the moment they sense it coming.
- **The badge is the terminal beat.** SETTLED / MOSTLY / HYPOTHESIS / NOBODY KNOWS, stamped
  in the final three seconds. Viewers will watch to the end specifically to see which one it
  is. **The honesty discipline is the completion mechanic.**
- **Land on the object, not a summary** — but see the "go outside and look" tic in the
  review findings. Six of twelve scripts used that exit; it is now capped.
- **Loop the timeline pieces.** GROW IT ends on the finished crystal and hard-cuts to the seed.
- **The only cliffhanger permitted is an unfinished piece of science.** No "part 2 in the
  comments." It looks like a small tactic and isn't: it makes incompleteness a manipulation,
  when the channel's whole position is that incompleteness is a *fact* reported honestly.

## Failure modes

**1. The renders become the point and the science becomes caption text.** The prettiest
asset always wins the edit. Six months in it is a wallpaper account with a voiceover, and
that audience cannot be converted back.
→ *Guardrail:* every piece names, in one sentence, the mechanism it teaches **before** any
asset is selected. If that sentence can't be written, there is no piece. And **no video's
visuals are renders alone** — every piece carries at least one 2D explanatory beat.

**2. The model gets mistaken for physics — first by the audience, then by the creator.**
This will not happen through dishonesty. It will happen in an edit, on a Tuesday, because
"in our model" is three extra words and the line scans better without them. Then again. After
six months the creator believes it too.
→ *Guardrail:* a persistent three-state source tag — **PHOTO / MODEL / DIAGRAM** — never
optional, never removed for a beauty shot. Plus the GROW IT rule: the timeline may only
illustrate a claim the corpus independently establishes.

**3. "Nobody knows" becomes a schtick.** Tier 4 is the best-performing shape and there are
only seven. Overspend it and the message inverts to "scientists don't really know anything"
— a rigor channel becoming a vibes channel without a single false statement being made.
→ *Guardrail:* one per month, hard cap; the 3:1 ending ratio, tracked; and **every "nobody
knows" must state which kind of open it is.** Difficulty and neglect look identical from
outside and are not the same thing. A tier-4 that doesn't make that distinction is doing the
damage.

**4. The uncited colourful detail.** Nakaya's rabbit hair. Kepler's poverty. Both already
flagged UNSOURCED — and both are exactly the eight-second beat that makes a script sing,
which is precisely why one will get spoken. A wrong detail on a two-million-view video is
permanent, indexed, and un-retractable.
→ *Guardrail:* no script enters production unless every factual beat carries a chapter
reference. Anything without one is **cut, not softened** — "some say" and "reportedly" are
how this failure launders itself. No exemption for "it's almost certainly fine."

**5. The backwards move eats itself.** By video 30 the audience predicts sentence two from
sentence one. The dangerous response is not fatigue but *manufacture* — finding an angle
that makes an ordinary fact sound inverted. That lie is invisible from outside and fatal
from inside, because the falsehood is in the framing rather than in any checkable statement.
→ *Guardrail:* the 40% cap handles fatigue. The manufacture is handled by the BACKWARDS
rule above, with no exceptions.

### Tactics to refuse

- **Engagement-bait cliffhangers** — makes incompleteness a manipulation.
- **"Scientists can't explain…"** — factually available and strategically fatal. Mystery
  channels cannot come back.
- **Staged retractions** — turns the most trust-dense format available into theatre.
- **Dropping "complex" from "no two alike"** — the slogan is only true with it. The shorter
  version is false, and it's the version that travels.
