## From the Libbrecht corpus

- **Split stars as the argument-ender for classification** (TAX1, arXiv:2109.00098v1) →
  Ch. 9. One simple growth process (capped column → two competing plates) produces several
  distinct final topologies; Kikuchi–Kajikawa's 121 types capture only some. A concrete
  worked example for the "no absolute classification" claim.
- **The substrate artifact, mechanistically** (TAX1) → Ch. 10. A hydrophilic ice/substrate
  contact line acts as a *permanent step source* that cheats the nucleation barrier —
  sharper than the current generic "substrate interactions" hedge.
- **Two missing-panel physics lessons** (TAX1 pp. 19, 23) → Ch. 7 or 10. The warm/high-σ
  corner of the observation grid is missing because growth is fast enough to *melt* the
  crystal; the cold/high-σ corner because a linear diffusion chamber physically cannot
  reach it.
- **Menagerie vocabulary from the needle atlas** (TAX1 Fig. 24) → Ch. 9. Tridents, cups
  with six side fins, I-beams, fishbones with retrograde sidebranches.
- **The restless surface, quantified** (CM1, arXiv:1211.5555v1) → Ch. 5. The ice surface
  recycles roughly one monolayer per *microsecond* while net growth is 4–6 orders of
  magnitude slower — a vivid number for the chapter's title concept.
- **CM1's falsifiable predictions** (arXiv:1211.5555v1) → Ch. 11/12. E.g. α_basal from
  vapour growth near 0 °C should match α_basal from liquid-water growth, since a thick
  quasi-liquid layer makes the two interfaces equivalent — which also explains why ice
  grown from liquid water facets on basal but not prism.
- **APP printed-equation erratum** (arXiv:1912.09440v1 p. 11) → Ch. 10 footnote if that
  page is ever quoted directly: the printed α expressions are missing their minus signs
  (verified against the page render); the correct form is α = A·exp(−σ₀/σ_surf).

## From Gravner–Griffeath 2009 (beyond the Ch. 8 addition)

- **"Butterflakes"** → Ch. 9. A predicted-but-never-observed morphology (thin plates
  growing orthogonally to the main plane), judged too brittle to survive a real cloud.
- **Circular markings as "reverse shapes"** → Ch. 8. Tip-nucleated single layers close
  around a near-circular hole that then shrinks — their explanation for circular surface
  markings on sandwich plates.
- **Drift vs. the hollow-prism-vestige ridge mechanism** → Ch. 8. They report little
  simulation evidence for the Nelson–Knight ridge-formation mechanism and attribute
  one-sided basal markings to drift.

## From the non-Libbrecht laboratory papers (beyond the implemented adds)

- **Free-fall double-plate statistics** (Takahashi 1991) → Ch. 8. All planar free-fall
  crystals were double plates falling small-plate-down; one plate wins the branching
  competition 43–76% of the time depending on temperature. (Ch. 9 got the summary; Ch. 8's
  ridge-writing discussion could use the fall-attitude detail.)
- **Trigonal/scalene seed artifact above −9 °C** (Takahashi 1991, citing Yamashita 1973)
  → Ch. 9, a tie-in to the triangle story: adiabatic-expansion seeds often come out
  scalene at −3.7 and −8.6 °C.
- **Harrison 2016 σ-calibration revision** (Pokrifka 2020 appendix) → Ch. 10. A worked
  example of a systematic: the 2016 chamber's low-supersaturation values were too high,
  and correcting them moved some inferred α values to near unity — measurement humility
  material.
- **Bailey–Hallett growth-rate parameterizations** → Ch. 13. Habit-frequency-weighted
  growth rates per unit supersaturation from −20 to −70 °C, validated against an
  orographic wave cloud — the application-facing side of the science.
- **Glossary entries** for the newly introduced terms: bullet rosette, kinetics
  transition, ventilation effect, sandwich instability, parallel crossed plates.
