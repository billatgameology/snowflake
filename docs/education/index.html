How a snowflake is made

A snow crystal is built entirely out of water vapour, by nothing but physics, with no plan and no template anywhere in the process. This is the whole story of how that works — from the shape of a single water molecule to the questions nobody has answered yet. Every claim is cited. Every diagram you can check.

How to read this site

Read it straight through

The chapters are in strict dependency order. Nothing is used before it is explained, so if you start at Chapter 1 and keep going you will never hit a word you have not already met. Close to four hours of reading end to end — longer if you stop to play with the animations, which you should. Parts I–III make a natural first sitting and IV–V a second.

Or jump to what you want

Each chapter opens with the question it answers and closes by handing you to the next one. If you only want to know why snowflakes have six sides, Chapter 3 is self-contained enough to read alone.

Two kinds of picture

Source figures come from the published papers and carry a strip naming the paper, figure and page. Original diagrams and animations are ours and carry a “built from” line naming the equation or measurement behind them. You can always tell which is which.

If a figure is missing, that is on purpose

The photographs and plots on this site belong to Kenneth G. Libbrecht, whose work is the source for nearly everything here. They are shown under citation and are deliberately not stored in this repository, because committing them would republish someone else’s copyrighted work. If you are reading a fresh copy without the local research cache, those figures appear as a labelled placeholder with the full citation intact — and everything else, including every animation, still works. FIGURES.md explains how to restore them.
