Part 1 of 2

Snowflake physics first

Snowflake growth is mostly a story about a gas, an interface, and a balance condition. This section teaches the ideas first; Part 2 shows how this repository turns them into update steps.

1) Thermodynamic framing: vapor <-> solid water

Start from the process statement: 

H2O(s) <-> H2O(g)

Libbrecht's framework keeps this as a single coupled process: transport sets the local supersaturation, the interface law converts that into a growth flux, and the growing boundary changes the transport field. The one-way behavior you see in this repo is an implementation policy for this educational path; the paper model keeps the coupled equilibrium logic explicit in both transport and surface terms.

2) What are rho and sigma? (do not skip this)

Think of one small grid cell as a tiny room.

  • rho = local vapor number density in the field (what is actually present in that room).
  • rhoSat(T) = balanced vapor density at temperature T: the density where local net flux can be zero if geometry and history are exactly balanced.

Their relationship is the local growth driving term: 

sigma = rho / rhoSat(T) - 1

Equivalent form:

sigma = (rho - rhoSat(T)) / rhoSat(T)

A small-σ approximation used in thermodynamic derivations is: 

Δμ / (kT) ≈ ln(1+sigma) ≈ sigma

This is the same normalization used in both `attachment-kinetics.md` and the papers:

  • sigma > 0: supersaturation, attachment-favorable.
  • sigma = 0: equilibrium.
  • sigma < 0: undersaturation in this formulation.

Numerical examples:

  • rhoSat(T)=100, rho=110 → sigma=0.10.
  • rhoSat(T)=100, rho=97 → sigma=-0.03.
  • rho=100 (same as rhoSat) → sigma=0.

In this project, positive sigma contributes to attachment demand through the interface law.

3) Why diffusion sits in the pre-step

In continuum form: 

d(rho)/dt = D * laplacian(rho)

In this simulator, each cycle treats diffusion as quasi-static: 

laplacian(rho) = 0

The boundary condition is not imposed by hand as a fixed formula. It is solved from:

  • far field condition (Dirichlet or reflecting), and
  • the surface exchange law derived from kinetics.

Libbrecht calls this link by the kinetic-length form:

X0(T,P) * (∂sigma/∂n)_surf = alphaHK * sigma_b

where X0 = (cSat/cIce) * D / v_kin and sigma_b is the boundary supersaturation from the neighboring vapor sample.

In plain language

Imagine every cell trying to average with neighbors. The solver asks for the fixed point of that diffusion interaction, then asks whether the interface coupling says how much ice should be added there.

4) Why kinetics is the hard part (and where habits come from)

The attachment law that connects supersaturation to shape change is: 

v_n = alphaHK * v_kin(T) * sigma_surf

with the exponential nucleation form: 

alphaHK(T, sigma_surf) = A(T) * exp(-sigma0(T) / sigma_surf)

This is the central nonlinearity: small changes in local supersaturation can produce large changes in growth speed. On broad basal and prism facets this is where temperature-dependent habit switching enters: where basal has much smaller alphaHK, prism wins and you get plates, and vice versa for columns. The monograph and phase-paper both identify this as the bridge between measured kinetics and morphology.

5) What this model keeps and what it omits

Kept from source-backed equations: quasi-static vapor solve, Kelvin-like interface coupling, the kinetic law, geometry-dependent facet classification, and deterministic fill accumulation. Deliberately limited here: full stochastic terrace nucleation, full latent-heat coupling, and full signed-terrace width dynamics are not treated in the baseline path.

That design choice is not a retreat; it is an explicit "what is in scope now" choice. Every missing term is called out and can become a future operator phase with its own ADR and protocol.

Animated microphysics sketch

Part 1 introduces the full equations from the papers in conceptual form; Part 2 shows how these are implemented in the discrete update cycle, including field solve convergence and checkpointed ledger semantics.

Hertz-Knudsen interface law (teaching form)

J_HK = alphaHK * v_kin(T) * cSat(T) * sigma_b

In this form, J_HK is flux into the surface: `alphaHK` is the attachment coefficient, `v_kin(T)` is the kinetic speed scale, `cSat(T)` is equilibrium vapor scaling, and `sigma_b` is boundary supersaturation.

Boundary fill proxy (concept shape only)

delta_fill = alphaHK * v_kin * sigma_b * dt / (hB * dx)

The interface state stores this as a dimensionless accumulator update. Each pixel is treated deterministically: fill increases, and the pixel becomes ice when accumulated fill reaches one. Physical uptake is measured as geometry-adjusted fill demand; relaxation exchange terms are explicitly tracked as diagnostics.

Part 2 of 2

How this simulator works

The simulator follows one explicit loop each step: compute field → quality checks → boundary growth → ledger and checkpoint.

One run step: what changes and what does not

  1. Build state: seed, active cells, walls, far-field, temperature, and controls.
  2. Solve vapor field: quasi-static Laplace-like solve on active lattice cells.
  3. Quality gates: residual and divergence are checked.
  4. Growth operator: either G-G machinery or LibbrechtKinetics boundary policy runs.
  5. Ledger + checkpoint: exact bytes are written for replay and evidence.

Residual

R = ||A*x - b||_inf

Residual is the largest mismatch in the solve equations. Smaller values mean the field changed to something close to equilibrium consistency.

Divergence

div = max abs(cellwise flux balance error)

Divergence checks whether the discrete flow is numerically budget-safe. For fixed-σ Dirichlet runs it is part of convergence policy; for reflecting LK runs it is a strict numerical diagnostic only.

Boundary mode truth table

  • fixed-σ Dirichlet: residual pass and divergence pass required to proceed.
  • Reflecting LK: residual must pass; divergence is recorded and flagged as diagnostic.

Mini-lab caveat

This panel is a teaching slice. It is not the production solver. It shows the order of operations and signal flow using simplified formulas.

Live outcomes

Shape tendency: plate with weak side branches

Growth style: stable medium pace

Branch detail: low-to-moderate branches

What to watch

  • Lower sigma_inf usually slows spread and can stall growth.
  • Higher alphaHK boosts attachment and fill rate.
  • Higher noise increases asymmetry and branch noise.
  • Fixed-σ mode is stricter to proceed than reflecting mode.

Why strict gates + checkpoints are required

Reproducibility first

Every accepted step stores exact state, operator tag, far field, temperature, and metadata. This enables deterministic replay and prevents accidental interpretation drift between runs.

Gate logic

Gates are not decorative. They formalize what is accepted as valid. Visual beauty and speed are never proof by themselves; they must be paired with numeric and metadata checks.

Reality check points

See active progress and protocol status in docs/PROGRESS.md, operator details in gg-machinery.md, kinetics in attachment-kinetics.md, and parameter tables in libbrecht-parameters.md.
