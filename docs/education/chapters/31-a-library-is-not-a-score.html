Chapter 31

A Library Is Not a Score

A century of experiments can teach us a great deal. But first we have to resist the tempting idea that every number belongs in the same spreadsheet column.

Guiding questionHow do you organize many unlike experiments without inventing a comparison they cannot support?

By the end, you can

  • Separate an evidence library from a leaderboard.
  • Explain why apparatus, observable, and history matter before two rows can be compared.
  • Read the three Phase 8 record roles without treating them as grades.
  • Explain what a source-blocked operand is.
What Phase 8 actually finished: a target book plus a successor evidence library containing 51 development records, 252,134 native data rows, and 431 digitized plot points. It intentionally contains no held-out Phase 7 evidence.

Fifty-one records are not fifty-one copies of one experiment

Imagine collecting reports from many kitchens. One cook records oven temperature every second. Another photographs the crust after an hour. A third changes the recipe halfway through. You have a rich library, but subtracting all the numbers from one model output would be nonsense.

Snow-crystal experiments differ in the same basic ways. Some measure growth speed. Some classify the final habit as plate-like or column-like. Some record a whole time history. Pressure, temperature control, background gas, crystal size, and the definition of “growth” can differ too.

First match the question, then compare the answer

A fair comparison needs compatible operands. An operand is simply one side of a calculation. If a paper reports a crystal’s diameter after 20 minutes, the matching model operand must define diameter the same way, use the matching conditions, and compare at 20 minutes. A final habit label is not a substitute for a growth-speed history.

This is why the Phase 8 library records more than numbers. It records what was measured, the apparatus and environment, the time or size interval, how the data were recovered, and what limits travel with them.

Why not pour everything into one score?

A single score feels decisive. It can also hide the most important scientific information. Ten thousand easy rows from one instrument could overwhelm five difficult habit observations from a different apparatus. A unit conversion might be legitimate for one family and impossible for another. Missing uncertainty cannot be repaired by averaging harder.

Phase 8 therefore built a typed library: records say what kind of evidence they contain. Later work can ask a narrow question of compatible records instead of pretending the whole archive is one giant test.

Sometimes the honest data value is “not recoverable”

Suppose a graph exists only in a source that the project cannot lawfully or reliably access. Or perhaps the paper gives the final curve but omits the calibration needed to turn pixels into physical units. The missing quantity is a source-blocked operand: the desired comparison is known, but one required input cannot be reconstructed from governed evidence.

That label is useful. It prevents a later model from quietly guessing a number, and it tells a human exactly what new source or measurement would unblock the comparison.

The handoff to model development

Phase 8 did not decide whether the simulator was good. It made future questions answerable and made some unanswerable questions visibly unanswerable. Phase 9 then took the next disciplined step: find the cheapest comparison that could reject a model proposal before spending months building it.
