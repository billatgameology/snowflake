Chapter 28

The Exam Result

Two hundred and four crystals, twenty hours, three agreements out of ninety — and not one invalid run. Which of those numbers is the result?

~18 min read 8 interactives 2 equations

By the end you will be able to

  • Hold two true statements about the same run at once: not one of its 204 crystals tripped a single numeric validity check, and it failed the map.
  • Say why the headline is 3 of 90 and not the 5 of 90 the same experiment measured first — and why the fall was written down before it happened.
  • Explain why passing named in-run checks narrows implementation suspicion without establishing composed numerical adequacy or proving a theory insufficient.
  • Distinguish a restricted equal-field coefficient-order diagnostic from a habit boundary, which only a 3-D forward run and the frozen evaluator may assign.
  • Recognise a project aiming its honesty machinery at its own good news.

Current Phase 6 status — corrected 2026-08-01

This chapter preserves the historical CAK story below, but the second historical arm has also finished. At the executed extent-21 configuration the measured-only results are CAK 3/90 and M1 54/90 on the common scope, 54/78 on M1's own scope. M1 is in-sample for Nakaya. Neither count is ADR 0026's registered conservative-intersection headline, and Phase 6 remains active and incomplete. Only ADR 0026’s conservative-intersection scoring rule is registered. R15 and M1_NO_DIP_ABLATION are planned but unfrozen, unimplemented and unexecuted; a composed numerical-adequacy campaign, the preview-budget GPU cohort and all four held-out families remain open. The 408 historical CPU-oracle rows do not satisfy the charter’s requirement for hundreds of automated preview-resolution runs on the GPU harness; no ADR amended that clause.

CAK→M1 changes the broad σ0 functions, prefactors, and dip factors together, so its score difference is confounded. M1 also substitutes an everywhere-narrow, temperature-only approximation; it does not implement the unresolved facet-width query. The width-dependent project M2 closure remains unimplemented. Only a future matched M1/no-dip pair, run under one frozen sampled configuration, could isolate the effect of the implemented dip-factor intervention in this solver. It could not by itself establish physical SDAK causality or necessity in nature.

Accepted ADR 0040 records corrected provenance without changing either executed artifact or measured class. It does not freeze R15 or retroactively make the old runs admissible to the replacement gate. That is distinct from the earlier CAK_A1 run below, which contradicted its own frozen protocol and was invalid as that registered experiment.

Two numbers came back

The exam was one command. node runner/src/main.ts phase6-sweep 6. It ran for about twenty hours on six worker processes and exited with status 0, having completed 204 of 204 points on the float64 CPU oracle — the slow reference engine, for the reason chapter 26 gave. Those lines are printed in the provenance table at the top of research/phase6-sweep-report.md, next to the fingerprint of the protocol it was run against — the twenty-five-row 8aeb2b80… that chapter 27 watched being sealed — the parameter set it ran, CAK, and two commit hashes with a verified relationship between them: the freeze commit is an ancestor of the commit that executed, which is the machine-checkable form of “the rules were written first”.

Two numbers came out, and they point in opposite directions.

The first is the score:

“Headline: 3 of 90 headline-scope points agree.”

research/phase6-sweep-report.md, “Scored result”

Three out of ninety — ninety being the counting points chapter 27 derived in advance, before anyone knew what would land on them. That is not a near miss. That is a miss so total that you would normally suspect the machine was broken.

The second number is the reason not to blame the numerics immediately:

“Zero invalid runs in 204, in both sweeps. Every point converged under the dual criterion, held symErr = 0 with noise off, kept every per-tick attachment delta D6h-invariant, and cleared the 65% domain-contact guard. Nothing was excluded, so nothing had to be argued about.”

research/phase6-sweep-report.md, “Findings” §2

Hold on to the phrase in both sweeps. There were two, the first of which this project threw away; the next section is that story. The four then-registered in-run checks passed in both, which made bookkeeping the first discovered failure. That does not establish the composed size/domain/grid/timestep adequacy still owed by R15.

At the time these Arm 1 artifacts were scored, four checks decided before either sweep applied automatically to every run. They live in one function in runner/src/phase6-sweep.ts called phase6ScorePoint: allConverged, deltaSymClean, symmetryError !== 0, domainContact. Fail any one and the run is stamped invalid and, in the file’s own words, “EXCLUDED BY NAME, never dropped — … Those are runs that did not happen properly, not statements about the model.” Decision 0035 later added two outcome checks to the current function: measured extent must reach 21, and a recorded configuration must say stopReason = "size-target". All 204 historical rows reached extent 21. They predate per-row configuration, so the second new check is disclosed as unavailable rather than fabricated after the fact.

None of them fired. Not once, across 204 crystals grown at 34 temperatures and six humidities. In plain English: every crystal passed both convergence criteria chapter 22 described, not just the iterate-residual check; every final crystal reported a symmetry error of exactly zero; and every per-tick attachment delta stayed perfectly six-fold symmetric. That last check is deliberately narrow: it does not certify the vapour field, fill field, or intermediate relaxation iterates. D6h is the crystallographer’s name for the symmetry of a hexagonal prism. No crystal ever grew far enough to touch the wall of its own box.

This is what makes the failure usable at its historical scope. None of the runs failed the four then-registered in-run checks. That narrows one immediate explanation; it does not turn those checks into a general certificate of numerical adequacy.

Chapter 15 taught you to separate two claims that sound alike, and the project’s own charter is blunt about why:

“Hold the distinction precisely, because it is the one this project exists to be honest about: giving a model real physics is not the same as showing the model reproduces reality.”

project charter.md, §1.5

So say it in the split form. The model was given a physically parameterized attachment rule with recorded P1/P2 provenance — measured or source-tabulated empirical inputs adopted directly are P1, including recorded textbook transport properties, while source fits, model inversions, project derivations, and figure digitizations remain P2. The model reproduces the Nakaya diagram — that claim is about outcome. Two historical frozen-scheme arms now supply measured-only results, but neither is the replacement registered headline or a Phase 6 conclusion. Independent verifiers have re-derived the historical results. The M1 arm later completed at 54/90 common scope and 54/78 arm scope, but both historical counts are measured-only and the replacement Phase 6 gate obligations remain open. Neither input provenance nor a historical score is allowed to stand in for validation.

Key idea

A morphology mismatch can be a scientific measurement only to the extent that its numerical and procedural validity are established. These 204 runs tripped none of the four validity rules recorded with their historical artifacts. The current extent guard also passes all 204; the absent historical stop-reason field is named as a provenance limit, not silently invented. That supports the narrower statement that no named historical invalidity was detected. It does not close the composed numerical-adequacy work required by R15, so the mismatch cannot yet be cleanly attributed to the parameterization’s physics alone.

Two things about the standing of that 3, before you read it anywhere else. Neither is a hedge added here; both are printed above the number in the report itself.

It is historical measured-only evidence, not the replacement gate. The historical report says in bold before it prints a single number, and records WP5, the project’s verification-and-closure work package, at its then-current state: “These numbers are not yet gate evidence. WP5 has not run: no independent verifier has re-derived report.json from points.json, and the six negative controls have not executed.” What it is instead, in the report’s own phrase, is “a measured result with verified provenance, which is not the same thing”. Chapter 27 built the distinction; this is it being applied to the project’s own headline.

Updated

That quote is the report’s wording as this page was first written. Subsequent work seven negative controls have executed, not six (app/scripts/phase6-wp5-negative-controls.mjs), and an independent verifier (app/scripts/phase6-wp5-independent.mjs) now re-derives all 204 points from points.json and reports PASS. That verifier first shipped doing too little — a follow-up audit found it printed the three artifact hashes without asserting them, checked one of report.json’s nine fields, and trusted a published pass/fail flag instead of recomputing it — and under those gaps it had certified two real forgeries as “VERIFIED…exit 0”: a substituted superseded-arm artifact, and a mutation that would have read the headline as 3 of 3 instead of 3 of 90. Commit 990840a hardened it: all three registered artifact digests are asserted; the exact 204-point grid is checked for duplicates, omissions, and additions; all nine report fields are re-derived; and ambiguity and domain-contact status are recomputed from raw values. It now correctly fails both forgeries. The controls’ own two real gaps — a splice from a different parameter set that was undetectable, and a run measured at the wrong crystal size that scored identically to a correct one — are addressed at commit b701285 by decision record 0035: future child output is checked against every pinned input, a size-target stop is required, and largestExtent ≥ 21 is enforced. Re-checking Arm 1 found all 204 rows at extent exactly 21, so the published classes and 3-of-90 headline do not move; the later code cannot add the per-row configuration and stop-reason fields those artifacts never recorded. hardened the historical artifact, but it did not create the replacement scientific gate. The current contract requires R15, numerical adequacy, a preview-budget GPU cohort, a matched M1/no-dip ablation, and held-out validation. Thus the accurate current label is measured-only, not a registered headline or completed Phase 6 gate.

And it is one historical arm of two. CAK is the broad-facet arm. The separately registered M1 execution later finished and measured 54/90 on CAK's common scope, or 54/78 after M1's pre-registered exclusions. That comparison is in-sample and confounded: CAK→M1 changes broad curves, prefactors, and dip factors together. The report's rule still applies: “The numbers below are complete and final for this arm; what they mean for the phase waits.” A matched M1/no-dip ablation and the other R15 obligations are still needed. So this chapter is reading historical results, not a completed Phase 6 verdict.

How the headline got corrected

The 3 replaced a 5, and how that happened is the most instructive thing on this page.

The first run of this same 204-point sweep finished, scored, and produced a headline of 5 of 90. Then an internal review compared the runs against the protocol they were supposedly run under, and found that they disagreed. The frozen protocol registered how one parameter should be built — aPrism, the prefactor curve for the prism faces, registered as "piecewise-linear-in-(Tm-T)", deliberately different from aBasal: "constant-1". Every one of the 204 runs had instead used Aprism ≡ 1 at every temperature, because the sweep harness never passed a parameter set to the child processes and a command-line default quietly supplied one. The decision that records it puts the mechanism in a single sentence:

“So an unregistered CLI default silently overrode a registered freeze row, through a path the freeze did not cover because paramSet was not itself a registered row.”

docs/decisions/0031-phase6-paramset-freeze-row.md

The charter clause that priced this was written long before anyone needed it: any post-freeze edit to parameters or protocol “invalidates prior sweep results — the full sweep re-runs”. So the 5 of 90 was not softened, annotated or kept as a secondary figure. It was invalidated — the decision insists on that word rather than “superseded” — and its artifacts were moved, unmodified, to a directory whose name carries its own obituary: evidence/phase6-sweep-6995868-cak-a1-superseded/. A full 204-point sweep’s worth of compute, thrown away by the project’s own rule, over a defect found by comparing the executed command path against the frozen rows.

Key idea

A missing rule and a broken rule are different failures. Chapter 27’s earlier amendments added rows nobody had thought to write down, and cost nothing because no experiment had run yet. This one was worse: the rule was written down, and the runs contradicted it — “a materially worse failure than an unregistered degree of freedom, because the artifact and the evidence disagree while both look complete”.

Now the part that makes this an experiment rather than a search. Before the re-run, the project worked out which way the headline would move, and wrote it down:

“Registered prediction: the headline falls from 5/90 to approximately 2/90.” … “The corrected result is expected to be worse, and that is the point. A validation whose headline improves when you fix a defect is pleasant; one whose headline degrades is the case that tests whether the pre-registration is real.”

docs/decisions/0031-phase6-paramset-freeze-row.md, Decision §4 and Consequences

Read why that sentence had to exist. Two parameter sets were available, one of which scored better. If the choice is made after seeing both scores, the pre-registration is worthless — it has become a post-registration. So the decision fixed the choice on provenance (the registered curve has a printed closed form behind it; the flat Aprism ≡ 1 has only a documented modelling convenience), fixed the expected direction in writing, and added the clause that closes the escape hatch: if the re-sweep returns a worse number, “that is the predicted outcome of adopting the better-provenanced inputs and is not grounds for revisiting this decision”.

The re-sweep came in at 3 of 90. The report grades its own forecast in one line: “Direction correct, magnitude one point pessimistic.” Here is the whole correction, row by row — all five of the original agreements sat at −2 °C, where the registered curve throttles the prism prefactor by 3.6×, and you can watch three survive it and two fail.

The forecast had been built by interpolating between two calibration probes and predicted a rise near 1.9–2.2×; the measured rises are 1.35–1.61×. The two-point interpolation ran hot, which is exactly why three agreements survived instead of two. The report also names what that says about the method rather than only about the answer: the rise is not monotone in f, so “a single throttle-to-rise factor was the wrong model for it; the probes happened to sample the two ends”. A prediction that lands one point off, for a reason you can state afterwards, is worth more than one that lands exactly and cannot say why.

One more thing the correction taught, and it is a physics result rather than a bookkeeping one. Restoring the registered curve made the prism faces harder to grow on, which is a push away from plates — and at the temperatures where that push is hardest the model did not become a column. It became nothing in particular. A probe at −5 °C, where the correction bites hardest of all, moved the aspect ratio from 0.3821 to exactly 1.0000: an equant crystal, as thick as it is wide. The findings document states the lesson in one line — “The broad-facet correction moves the model from one habit to no habit, not to the other habit” — and you will meet that same shape again twice on this page.

The measured diagram

The sweep is a grid: 34 temperatures from −2 to −35 °C one degree apart, crossed with six humidity levels expressed as a fraction f of water saturation — 0.10, 0.15, 0.25, 0.40, 0.60 and 0.90. Thirty-four times six is 204. Each cell is one crystal, grown from a 19-site hexagonal seed until it reached a registered measurement size, then measured for its aspect ratio — thickness divided by width, so a low number is a flat plate and a high number is a stubby column — and sorted into one of three classes: plate (aspect ratio at or below 0.6667, which is 1/1.5), column (1.5 or above), or neutral — the model declining to commit to either. Those two numbers are the registered ones, spelled out in runner/src/phase6-protocol.ts as “AR = z-extent / T-extent; plate AR ≤ 1/1.5 (0.6667), column AR ≥ 1.5, otherwise neutral”.

Chapter 27 sealed the marking scheme before any of this ran — before either run of it, which is worth saying now that there have been two. Three of its clauses do all the work here. Neutral counts as disagreement, because the reference names a habit in every regime and the neutral band is the model’s invention, not the reference’s. Points within 1 °C of a reference boundary do not count, in either direction. And the coldest regime, which accepts both plates and columns, is reported but kept out of the headline, because a band that accepts both answers cannot discriminate between them. Ninety points survive those rules. Three of them agree.

Where those three sit matters more than the fact that there are three. In the temperature band where the reference demands columns, the model agreed on 0 of 24 counting points; in the band where it demands cold plates, 0 of 60. The denominators differ because the bands are different widths on the temperature axis, and each surviving temperature carries six humidities with it.

Its one-directional premise was later refuted by the project’s own size ladder, where one measured aspect ratio fell from 1.52632 to 1.52174. You can check the class totals off the grid above by hand, because it is the report’s own character grid transcribed cell for cell — and the 168 is the model refusing to pick a side, on more than four points in every five.

Do the same count on the withdrawn run — switch the interactive across and read it off the grid — and it was 31 plate, 143 neutral, 30 column. Restoring one registered curve cost the model twenty-five of its plates and bought it no columns at all: the column count is identical, because the correction barely reaches the cold end. The registered prefactor climbs to 1 as it gets colder — the source’s own figure caption says both prefactors are about 1 between −10 and −30 °C — so from about −15 °C down the two parameter sets very nearly coincide. Be careful with how far that goes, because an earlier version of the report said the two were flatly “the same model” below −10 °C, and a 2026-07-29 audit measured that and found it false: Aprism is 0.830 at −10 °C and only reaches 1 at −15, and 11 of the 72 points in the cold plate band do differ between the two runs, by up to 0.109 in aspect ratio. What is true is the part the 0 of 60 rests on: not one of those 72 points changed class. And that is the second sighting of the shape: away from one habit, not toward the other.

How we know

Each measured grid is published as 34×6 characters of plain text, small enough to print in a report and read with your eyes. That is a deliberate choice: it means any reader can re-run the scoring rule by hand and arrive at 3 of 90 without trusting the program that produced it. The corrected grid is the one in research/phase6-sweep-report.md today; the withdrawn grid is in the same file at the revision it was withdrawn from, commit 6995868 in the project’s history, with its raw artifacts preserved unmodified at evidence/phase6-sweep-6995868-cak-a1-superseded/ — so a withdrawn result stays checkable instead of vanishing. The CAK correction-comparison interactive reads from those two tracked point sets; other interactives have their own stated fixtures. Re-implementing the registered rule over them reproduces the published per-band totals — 3/6 warm plates, 0/24 columns, 0/60 cold plates — and, over the withdrawn grid, 5/6, 0/24, 0/60. Both headlines come out exactly as published.

All three wins at one temperature

Now the part where the honesty machinery turns on the project’s own good news. All three agreements are at the same temperature. The report does not bury that; it prints it in bold, immediately under the headline:

“All three agreements are at −2 °C, the single counting temperature in the warmest regime — which ADR 0025 registered pre-sweep as carrying essentially no statistical weight, precisely so this could not be presented as a result. The model agrees with the reference nowhere else inside the headline scope.”

research/phase6-sweep-report.md, “Scored result”

Read the timing carefully, because it is the whole point. The warm plate band spans temperatures warmer than −3.3 °C. On a one-degree grid starting at −2 °C, and with the ±1 °C ambiguity band removing −3 °C, exactly one temperature in that band can ever count. The scoring decision therefore wrote this down before either sweep, while the outcome was still unknown:

“A stated limitation rather than a discovered one: the warmest Plates regime has exactly ONE counting temperature (−2 °C). It can therefore only ever score 0% or 100% and carries essentially no statistical weight… Any conclusion about the warm plate regime is a one-point conclusion and must say so.”

docs/decisions/0025-phase6-agreement-scoring-rule.md, Consequences

The “0% or 100%” in that clause is loose, and worth correcting in passing rather than repeating: the single counting temperature carries six humidity cells, which can and do disagree with each other, so the band can land anywhere from 0 to 6. It scored 5 of 6 in the withdrawn run and 3 of 6 in this one. What the clause was actually protecting is untouched by that, and it is the part to keep: any conclusion about the warm plate band is a one-temperature conclusion and must say so.

So every CAK headline agreement in these two historical sweeps is a one-temperature conclusion, and the rule that says so was sealed before either sweep. At that one temperature, three of the six humidity cells came out plate, which is what the reference asks for; the other three came out neutral, which the rule counts as a miss. That is the entire positive half of the headline: one temperature, three humidities — and one of those three is flagged extent-fragile, meaning it sits close enough to the plate ceiling that measuring the crystal at a slightly different size could move it out of the class. In the withdrawn run the same band had five of six. No other headline-scope point agreed in either of those two historical runs.

And then the honesty machinery does something less expected: it fires in the other direction, and takes back an accusation the project had made against itself. Earlier versions of this record put the warm end under suspicion, on the grounds that the digitized prism σ0 curve — the one the whole warm end rests on — sat low by a factor of 1.6 to 3.2 against a closed form Libbrecht had printed. A closer reading reversed it. The current report labels the paragraph CORRECTED and explains that the dedicated source papers report fitted or model-inferred P2 values of σ0,prism = 0.03% at −2 °C and about 0.2% at −5 °C, that the project’s digitized curve matches those to roughly 7%, and that the closed form it had been judging itself against is the outlier of the three, high by 3.01×. Its conclusion: “The warm end does not rest on a suspect σ0,prism.” That is a source-consistency conclusion, not a claim that the modeled barrier or surface supersaturation was directly measured.

Notice that this is a withdrawn criticism, not a withdrawn result, and it is reported with the same machinery and the same bold labels as the withdrawn headline was. A record that only ever corrects downward is not a record, it is a mood. The undercut that survives is the one that was registered before the sweep: one counting temperature, no statistical weight, and it says so itself.

Key idea

Anyone can be honest about their bad news. The test of an honesty system is whether it fires on the good news too — and here the same document that reports three agreements is the document that says, in bold, that all three sit at one temperature it had already registered as carrying essentially no weight, and that one of the three is fragile to how big the crystal was when it was measured.

−5 °C, and a claim withdrawn

There is a second result at −5 °C, and the honest way to tell it is to start with the version of it this page used to carry.

Begin with what is not in dispute. At −5 °C the Nakaya diagram demands a column, and the corrected run produced no column there — all six of its humidity cells at that temperature came out neutral. But −5 °C is also the subject of a dedicated laboratory paper by Libbrecht himself, and on the bench he found the opposite of the diagram. His substrate experiments there produced aspect ratios “throughout the range 0.1 < ρaspect < 1. Columnar crystals (with ρaspect > 1) were absent”, with many crystals “blockier, nearly isometric” and thick plates “the norm” at a typical ρ of about 0.5 — and on p. 10 he draws the conclusion himself: “Producing columnar crystals at −5 C then requires the SDAK effect.” So at this one temperature the diagram and its own author’s bench disagree with each other.

An earlier version of this chapter read that as the model taking the bench’s side. It quoted two numbers — AR 0.3821, measured at −5 °C under the parameter set that was later withdrawn, and exactly 1.0000, what the registered set produces at the same conditions — noted that both sit at or inside his measured band with no column in sight, and called that a reproduction of his laboratory measurements. On 2026-07-29 the project withdrew the claim, against its own interest, for the plainest reason available: it was built on one cell out of six.

Three further things came out with the withdrawal, and all of them are the kind of detail that decides whether a comparison was ever clean. The first is that the two quantities may not even be the same measurement. His ρaspect is H/R, half-thickness over effective radius; the halves cancel, so it equals the model’s AR — but only if his R is the circumradius. The model’s width is the corner-to-corner diameter of a hexagon, and if “effective radius” means an equal-area radius instead, his ρ runs about 10% larger than the model’s AR. He does not define it in that sentence, so the equivalence is unverified rather than established. The second is that his own paper is not one-sided about columns at all: it reports columns at −5 °C in free fall, and on a substrate too when the experiment is started at a high enough far-field supersaturation. His position is conditional on growth history, and quoting only the no-columns sentence made it look flat. The third is that his bench was not running the model’s experiment anyway: he nucleated a crowded field of crystals and let them grow together, so that diffusion between neighbours pulls the surface supersaturation down, while the model grows one isolated crystal with a monopole-matched far field. Crowded and isolated growth are different diffusion problems.

Two things about the standing of these numbers, both of which would have applied just as forcefully if the row had matched him.

First, the −5 °C laboratory data is upstream of the model’s own inputs. The attachment-kinetics curves the model runs on were built from Libbrecht’s measurement programme, and this paper is part of that programme. Agreement here could never have been independent validation; the most it could ever have been is a consistency check on the pipeline from his printed measurements to a grown 3-D crystal. Which makes the withdrawal cost less than it looks: what has been lost is a strong internal check, not a piece of external evidence. The sweep report and the figure-findings section cited under the table are the documents that measured the row; later interpretation records must preserve the withdrawal rather than revive the old claim.

Second, none of these numbers enters the replacement gate. The six cells in the table come from the historical CAK sweep, whose independent re-derivation passes. Accepted ADR 0040 preserves them as measured-only while requiring a full replacement rerun. AR 1.0000 as the old reading quoted it came from a calibration probe: a run made to help choose settings, not a run made under the frozen protocol. The project maintains a two-tier truth system for exactly this reason, and the document that prints the number prints the restriction beside it: “These are CALIBRATION PROBES and are never citable as gate evidence.” And AR 0.3821 was a swept value — from the run that has since been invalidated.

What survives at this temperature is smaller than an agreement, and it is still worth having. The diagram wants a column here. Its own author’s bench found plates, and he wrote in print that producing columns at −5 °C requires the mechanism this arm does not contain. The model, run without that mechanism, produced neither habit. So the 0 of 24 in the columns band is a miss against a target the source itself qualifies — which is a weaker claim than this section used to make, and one that the measured row actually supports.

Key idea

Three withdrawals sit on this one page, and the pattern is the point. A headline was withdrawn and replaced with a worse one. A criticism the project had made against itself was withdrawn as unfounded. And here an agreement — the single most quotable good thing in the whole result — was withdrawn because it rested on one cell out of six. A record that can only revise in the direction that flatters it is not a record.

Three investigation lanes

A model disagrees with a reference. Three useful places to investigate are the implementation, the inputs, and the model assumptions. They are not an exhaustive theorem, and Part Two does not eliminate the first two universally. It records named validity checks, provenance tests, and limitations that narrow which explanations remain plausible at the executed configuration.

Try it yourself. Pick a culprit and see what comes back.

The historical evidence against an immediate code-failure explanation is not just “zero invalid runs”. The planned gate also separates evidence validity from a morphology miss: disagreement with Nakaya is reportable rather than something the evaluator may tune away. The plan writes that separation as executable behaviour:

“A morphology miss is not a gate failure: the gate fails on invalid numerics, provenance, artifacts, incomplete runs, or a protocol violation — never on the model disagreeing with the diagram. That separation is what makes ‘a negative result is a result’ executable rather than aspirational.”

docs/plans/phase-6-nakaya-validation.md

The support for the input provenance used to have two parts, and the loud one has just been withdrawn: the −5 °C row does not reproduce his bench. What is left is the quiet part, a blind extraction cross-check about one specific quantity: the prefactor curve Aprism — the ceiling on how sticky a prism face can get in this parameterization — was digitized off a printed figure, pixel by pixel, before anyone found the later paper that prints the same curve as an equation — arXiv:2009.08404v2, Eq. (5). Compared afterwards, the digitized Aprism anchors reproduce the printed formula to 8.4% at worst and under 2% typically. An extraction that agrees with a source it could not have peeked at is an independent cross-check of that extraction, not direct measurement of the modeled surface state. And note which run that check covers: it clears the registered curve, and the withdrawn 204 runs did not use it — they used Aprism ≡ 1 at every temperature. The evidence and the evidence’s subject only lined up once the sweep was re-run, which is one more way of saying why the re-run had to happen.

So the check is stamped “with scope” rather than clean, and the scope has moved in both directions since it was first written. It grew: the suspicion over the warm prism σ0 has itself been withdrawn, as the section on the three agreements described. It also shrank, in the section just above: the blind extraction check clears one curve, not the whole input set, and the −5 °C comparison that used to back it up no longer does. What remains is the limitation that will not go away — the kinetic curves relevant to this comparison trace back to one physicist’s programme, often through a P2 fit, model inversion, project derivation, or figure digitization. Agreement against upstream material from that same programme is therefore a consistency check rather than held-out validation, and at −5 °C the model does not manage even that.

Careful

“Zero invalid runs” does not mean “zero errors”. It means zero runs tripped four named validity conditions. The Aprism substitution tripped none of them, because no automatic check was watching that row: an unregistered command-line default quietly overrode a frozen parameter, and all 204 affected runs still passed the four named checks. A machine can only catch the mistakes somebody thought to name — and this one was caught by a reader comparing the runs against the protocol, not by the machine.

The corrected run carries a second example, found while it was running and recorded in the report as not yet fixed at that snapshot. The harness stamps the provenance once at the start, but each of the 204 child processes launches the model from the working tree — so an edit to the solver mid-run would change the physics for the later points while the report still named the commit it started from. Nine commits from a parallel session did land during the twenty hours; the project checked afterwards that none of them touched the solver, so this sweep is unaffected. Its own verdict on that: “That was luck, not design.” At the time, the completion-time source re-check was outstanding; the update immediately below records the later forward fix.

Updated

The outstanding fix named just above has since landed. A commit dated 2026-07-29 (after this page’s own text was written) adds a hash of every source file under core/src, solver-cpu/src and runner/src, taken before the first child launches and taken again after the last one finishes; the sweep now throws rather than publishing if the two endpoint fingerprints disagree. It can detect a commit that remains landed or an edit present at an endpoint, but not a transient reverted edit, inherited NODE_OPTIONS, or a shadow module. R15 still owes an immutable source snapshot, an environment allowlist and verified child identity. The endpoint check cannot reach backward and re-verify Arm 1, whose provenance still rests on exactly the one-off diff this page just described. The project’s own words for that stand unedited: that was luck, not design; what changed is that the next arm does not need luck.

What curve equality can show

An earlier version of this chapter promoted an input-curve crossing into a habit boundary. That inference is retracted. A snow crystal's class comes from a coupled 3-D forward run: basal and prism faces see different local surface fields, geometry and history, and the frozen evaluator measures the published artifact. A two-curve calculation cannot represent the neutral class that dominates the CAK result.

The restricted calculation is still useful if it is named precisely. At the same positive σsurf, compare alphaHKBasal and alphaHKPrism, where alphaHK = A·exp(−σ0/σsurf). Its outputs are basal-higher, prism-higher, or a tie. They are input-function diagnostics, never plate/column labels, habit transitions, or a Nakaya score. If the two prefactors are also equal, coefficient equality reduces to equality of the two σ0 functions.

Under the project's registered, Figure-1-width-supported P4 base-10 transcription, the dip-bearing functions drawn below have three such restricted σ0-equality locations: −3.08, −8.07, and −24.73 °C. These are not dip centres and not morphology boundaries. The source writes log without specifying its base. The dip centres are −4.5 and −14.4 °C. A different logarithm-base transcription can alter the effective width and the equality roots, but cannot move either centre: at T = Tcentre, log(T) - log(Tcentre) = 0 in every logarithm base.

σ0,basal(T*) = 0.02·T*1.75 + 0.3
σ0,prism(T*) = 0.02·T*1.9 − 0.025(T* − 0.3)

where
T*
how far below freezing, in degrees C, written as a positive number — so T* = 15 means −15 °C
σ0
a fitted nucleation-barrier scale for a facet, in percent; by itself it does not assign a 3-D habit
basal / prism
the flat top-and-bottom faces, and the six side faces, of a hexagonal ice prism

Printed closed forms, arXiv:2009.08404v2 p. 3, Eqs. (2) and (3), transcribed verbatim in research/libbrecht-figure-findings.md §2

Those two printed source fits form the approximate mode’s broad curves. The registered M1 mode uses the same basal fit and the later paper’s different printed prism fit before applying its dip factors. Publication does not turn either fit into a direct measurement: under the project’s taxonomy they are P2 inputs. Use the widget to inspect curve order and equality, not to make habit boundaries.

The approximate mode can show five equalities; that is only a property of this page's own notch. In the registered P4 base-10 mode, with both dips at their published centres, the restricted equality count is three at −3.08, −8.07, and −24.73 °C. The numbers resemble the reference boundary count, but resemblance is not a morphology result. The separately re-derived historical outcomes are CAK 3/90 and M1 54/90 common scope (54/78 arm scope), and M1's agreement is in-sample.

The source’s exact M1 functional forms, depths, denominator constants, and placement used for Nakaya comparison are substantially uncertain and P3 Nakaya-informed. Its base-unspecified log is separate: the project’s Figure-1-width-supported base-10 transcription is P4. Published model-dependent inversions of narrow-facet growth observations support barrier reductions near the approximate temperature regions, but they do not directly measure σsurf or verify this exact M1 form. A dip-bearing match to Nakaya is therefore in-sample reproduction, never independent validation.

Still unsettled

Even a correctly evaluated coefficient-order diagnostic cannot establish a morphology boundary. The forward solver has facet-dependent fields, geometry, size, and seed history. M1 further assumes every active facet is narrow instead of querying width, while the width-dependent project M2 closure remains unimplemented. A future R15 path must obtain habit labels from published 3-D artifacts. Its matched M1/no-dip pair can isolate only the implemented intervention effect under the frozen sampled configuration, not physical SDAK causality or necessity.

The knob that helps nothing

The obvious objection to all of this is: you just did not tune it well enough. The most plausible knob is the starting seed — every row in these historical sweeps starts from the same little 19-site hexagon, radius 2, thickness 1, which is itself plate-shaped. Does the model grow plates because the physics says so, or because it started as one?

That was measured, not argued. Five seed geometries were run at the two temperatures that discriminate: −5 °C, where the reference demands a column (AR at or above 1.5), and −15 °C, where it demands a plate (AR at or below 0.6667). The answer is more interesting than a flat “no”.

One disclosure first, because these ten runs predate the correction: all of them were run under the parameter set that was later withdrawn. At −15 °C that changes nothing — that is the temperature at which the registered Aprism has climbed to 1, which is the value the withdrawn set used everywhere, so the two are the same model at exactly that point and those five numbers stand as they are. At −5 °C the registered curve would shift the whole column of five upward, as it shifted everything else at that temperature. The conclusion below survives that shift because it turns on direction, not on the values: a seed pushes both temperatures the same way, and the two bands need opposite pushes.

Seed shape is not a small knob. It moves the aspect ratio by +0.41 at −5 °C and +0.51 at −15 °C between the registered seed and a needle-like one, and the needle-like seed does produce a genuine column — at −15 °C, where a plate is required. That is the honest, awkward version of the result, and the project states it plainly rather than claiming the knob is weak.

Within this five-seed, two-temperature probe, a fixed seed moved both points in the same direction while the two targets needed opposite moves. The seed that comes closest at −15 °C is the worst one at −5 °C; the seed that comes closest at −5 °C manufactures the wrong habit at −15 °C. Not one of the five agrees at either temperature.

Key idea

In this five-seed probe, changing seed shape moved both tested temperatures in the same direction and rescued neither target. That measured result is scoped to the five seeds, two temperatures, CAK-era configuration, and executed size. It is not a theorem about all diffusion, grids, far fields, domains, or parameterizations.

The shape of the failure

A score tells you the model missed. It does not tell you how. Two facts about the shape of the miss came out of the historical CAK sweep. A July 2026 literature check did not locate the same reported analysis, but that scoped search does not establish universal priority.

The first is that, in this CAK artifact, the diagnostic curve-equality location is not the measured morphology transition. Plate growth ends at −4 °C in the driest row, −3 °C in the next and −2 °C in the one after that — nowhere near the registered −10 °C σ0-equality diagnostic — and then columns do not begin until −19 °C or, at the next humidity up, −23 °C. The band in between, 14 to 19 °C wide depending on the row, is the model declining to commit to either habit, and it is the single largest feature of the measured diagram. On Nakaya’s side of the comparison there is no gap at all: a knife-edge boundary at −9.9 °C.

The correction widened that gap rather than closing it, which is the third sighting of the same shape. In the withdrawn run plate growth had persisted to −8 or −9 °C across four humidity rows and the neutral band was roughly 10 °C wide. Restoring the registered curve pulled the plate edge back to −4/−3/−2 °C, left the column edge exactly where it was, and so made the region of no habit at all bigger. Drag the humidity slider below and you are reading the corrected run; the grid interactive higher up will show you the other one.

The second measured pattern is that the historical CAK artifact loses classified habit in its wetter rows. Columns appear only in the two driest rows — and at f = 0.40, 0.60 and 0.90 the model produces nothing but neutral across all 34 temperatures. Not a plate, not a column, at any temperature from −2 to −35 °C, in half the humidity range. The pre-run equal-field input diagnostic anticipated narrowing facet contrast as the far-field proxy rose; the forward artifact separately measured the all-neutral rows. The diagnostic does not identify the cause of that morphology. In the withdrawn run only the wettest row had been that empty; the corrected run empties three.

alphaHK = A · exp(−σ0 / σsurf)

where
alphaHK
the attachment coefficient: the fraction of molecules striking this facet that actually stick
A
the facet’s prefactor, its stickiness ceiling
σ0
the facet’s critical supersaturation — low means sticky
σsurf
the supersaturation actually present at the surface, which rises as you make the air wetter

As printed in research/phase6-sweep-report.md, “Findings” §5; current interpretation restricts this to an input-function diagnostic

Under the deliberately restricted comparison, increasing one shared positive σsurf drives each coefficient toward its own ceiling A. Evaluated on the registered input curves with the far-field proxy, the basal/prism contrast spans 0.34 to 3.76 across temperature at f = 0.15 and 0.84–1.25 at f = 0.90. That was computed before either sweep and motivated retaining the top humidity row. It is not a mechanism assignment: the coupled solver gives different local fields, geometry, and history to different facets, and a restricted coefficient ratio cannot label habit. Independently, the published CAK artifact measures neutral classes across the three wettest rows while the reference diagram is highly structured there. Their juxtaposition motivates a hypothesis for the matched replacement campaign; it does not establish why the morphology collapsed.

Both facts sharpen the same lesson: restricted coefficient-order equality and a 3-D habit-transition location are different observables. Only the latter is what the frozen morphology evaluator records.

What this does not show

The sweep report ends with a list of what its own result does not establish, and any honest retelling has to carry that list rather than the headline alone.

It is measured-only evidence, and it is not a phase conclusion. The report's historical text says that no verifier and six controls were then owed. Current history is different: hardened independent readers pass, and both historical 204-row arms completed (CAK 3/90; M1 54/90 common and 54/78 arm scope). They are not the registered conservative-intersection headline. R15 and M1_NO_DIP_ABLATION are planned but unfrozen, unimplemented and unexecuted; composed numerical adequacy, the preview-budget GPU cohort and all four held-out families remain open.

Historical verification update. The Arm 1 verifier asserts all three artifact digests, re-derives the exact 204-row set and all report fields, and rejects the two real forgeries its first version accepted. The seven executed controls found two real gaps; decision 0035 pins inputs and the size-target outcome for future runs, while Arm 1 remains historically honest about the per-row fields it lacks. None of that closes the replacement scientific gate. The fuller historical account, with file names and what each gap was, is earlier in this chapter. Current authority is docs/HANDOFF.md, docs/PROGRESS.md, and the science-first Phase 6 plan, not the old audit-cutoff snapshot.

The CAK material on this page is not a test of M1. Every CAK grid shown in the historical interactives is a no-dip Arm 1 run. The report’s historical wording — “SDAK appears in no source file” — was true of the execution it describes. M1 later completed at 54/90 common scope and 54/78 arm scope. That result is in-sample, and CAK→M1 is confounded; it is not a causal dip-factor ablation.

Around −5 °C, the two-axis single-label score is underdetermined by the reported observations. The source reports that there “both platelike and needlelike crystals can grow under essentially identical conditions”. Temperature and humidity alone therefore do not select one observed label in that band; unrecorded history or other variables may matter. A one-mark-per-grid-point scheme must carry that ambiguity rather than claim a unique natural answer. The report lists this among the things producing columns would require: a fast-growth transient the model has no representation of, alongside a nucleation barrier whose controlling terrace width is about 50 nanometres against this grid’s 350-nanometre cells.

The comparison target is a redrawn 1954 schematic. Its supersaturation axis failed an independent check when the project digitized it, which is why only its three boundary temperatures are used and its humidity values never are. No observational habit boundary anywhere carries a numerical uncertainty; the ±0.5 °C the project uses is its own construction.

The historical class is reported at the executed configuration. Earlier reconnaissance sampled separate axes and did not compose fixed-physics size, domain, grid, and timestep convergence. It therefore does not establish R15 numerical adequacy. The old class and aspect ratio remain measurements at their named size and domain, not extrapolated continuum claims.

The cross-platform control was limited. The preserved fixtures self-report win32/x64 and darwin/arm64 hosts on the same Node/V8 build; those headers are not independent hardware authentication. Tier 1 library-math fingerprints differed in 9 of 448 entries (1–31 ULP), but cover only integer −2 through −30 °C plus three boundaries; the registered −31 through −35 °C tail is absent. A historical Tier 2 table reports matching rows at four CAK points, but the raw arm64 logs and exit records were never tracked, so those rows are not independently rederivable evidence. No CAK transfer, digit-level, M1, or full-grid portability claim follows. The correction had voided the old control: the two-point fixture meant to catch a habit class flipping between machines was built around −5 and −15 °C, and under the registered parameter set both of those come out neutral — the calibration probe’s AR 1.0000 and about 1.1053 — and a two-point test whose two points share a class cannot detect a class flip.

It has since been rebuilt, in the order that keeps it a test. The decision that replaces it registered the rule for picking new points while the re-sweep was still running and its results did not exist yet — because choosing fixture points after seeing which ones happened to land near a class boundary would be selecting the test from the answers, which is the same freedom the parameter-set correction was written to remove. Applied afterwards, the rule returns four points in two pairs. The robust pair is the smallest and largest aspect ratio in the whole sweep — −2 °C at AR 0.263158, a clear plate, and −28 °C at 2.33333, a clear column — and it asks whether the pipeline agrees across architectures at all. The fragile pair sits as close to the class thresholds as anything in the sweep — −3 °C at 0.684211, just over the plate ceiling, and −23 °C at exactly 1.5, one lattice site from changing class — and it asks whether any habit class here was decided by a coin toss in the last bit. The two pairs are reported separately and never pooled, and a difference in either is registered in advance as a finding rather than a bug to be tuned away. Their x64 baseline was published from the corrected sweep, and a later prose table was attributed to a fixture that self-reported darwin/arm64. That label is not hardware authentication, and the missing raw output prevents independent rederivation; R15 must publish normalized Tier 2 output and exit records and fingerprint the full frozen temperature grid. It does not establish general cross-platform portability or any M1 result.

And habit is measured at one crystal size, and habit is size-dependent. The historical one-sided CAK flag marks 16 rows, including one of the three agreements. The later symmetric, boundary-inclusive audit counts 59 CAK rows within the same 0.135 distance of either class threshold — 43 more than the historical flags — and 85 M1 rows, 52 more than its old flags. This is a fragility census, not evidence that any particular row changes class at another size; the composed R15 size study remains open.

One number on this page was already withdrawn once, and so was one claim. The 3 of 90 is what stands; the 5 of 90 is what the same experiment measured before the project invalidated it, and it is quoted here only as the thing that got corrected. Neither number should ever be quoted bare. The claim is the −5 °C one: that the sweep reproduced Libbrecht’s own laboratory measurements there, withdrawn on 2026-07-29 once the whole six-cell row was read instead of one cell of it. What the withdrawals cost, and what is still owed, is chapter 29’s.

No human domain-expert review is recorded. Every recorded non-maker reviewer-agent round — including the recorded two-reviewer process that checked this page — is an LLM review. Maker audits are recorded separately, and the maker is not identified as a domain scientist. The project’s own sharing rules require that at least one human domain scientist read any manuscript before submission, and treat the absence of a recorded review as a disclosed limitation, not a substitute. Phase 6 remains active and incomplete as this is written. A Phase 3 browser development instrument already exists — solver in a worker, overlays, vapour slice, picking, and readouts. Phase 7, the polished product layer and its audience profiles, has not started.

What is left, after all of those are subtracted, is small and durable. A model built from one physicist’s published empirical inputs, source fits, model inversions, and project digitizations was pointed at the most famous picture in his field, under a marking scheme sealed in advance, and CAK missed. A scoped literature search found no matching 3-D grid report, but that search does not establish universal priority. Keep the two halves apart: a miss was a source expectation; what the CAK miss looks like — the fourteen-to-nineteen-degree gap where the model has no habit at all, the habit destroyed by humidity — is this project’s, and it is the smaller of the two. At −5 °C, the one temperature where his own laboratory data and the diagram contradict each other, it missed both of them: no habit at all where the diagram wants columns and his bench found plates. And it missed at the conditions actually run. No curve-order theorem establishes that every broad-facet parameterization must fail. What survives is narrower: the historical CAK forward run, at its executed size and domain, measured 3/90 and did not reproduce the diagram. M1 measured 54/90 common scope, in-sample, under a confounded bundled change. The replacement gate must decide what can be claimed after its numerical, GPU, ablation, and held-out obligations.

A historical findings-ledger phrase called the CAK result “the signature of a correct implementation of an insufficient theory”. Read it as a scoped interpretation of the named checks and executed parameterization, not as proof of implementation correctness or of universal theoretical necessity.

Which leaves the bill. Twenty hours of compute for the run that stands, an entire earlier sweep of the same size thrown away to get it, every phase of machinery that came before both, plus verifier hardening and adversarial controls. A historical prose table reports four CAK rows attributed to a self-reported darwin/arm64 fixture, but that header is not hardware authentication and its Tier 2 raw output and exit records are absent. The historical M1 sweep has run. R15, composed numerical adequacy, the preview-budget GPU cohort, matched M1/no-dip evidence, and held-out validation remain owed — that is the next chapter, along with the list of things this project still does not know.
