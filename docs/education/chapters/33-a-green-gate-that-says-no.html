Chapter 33

A Green Gate That Says No

A program checks a scientific record and lights up green. The science inside the record failed. How can both be true, and what does the green light actually certify?

~17 min read6 interactives0 solver runs in this phase

By the end you will be able to

  • Separate a closure-gate pass from a scientific pass, and say what each one certifies.
  • Explain what an unresolved operand does to a proposed evidence bridge, using the six real branches.
  • Read a numerical ladder with a registered criterion without averaging away the failed rows.
  • Describe how a project’s assurance machinery can consume its experiment, and name the rule that was sharpened afterwards to stop it.
  • Tell “refused”, “incomplete”, “not executed” and “no verdict” apart, and say why the difference matters.

Where this chapter sits — Phase 10, complete-negative 2026-08-25

Phase 10 lasted five days. Its closure verifier passed 7 of 7 checks. No solver ran, no target or habit row was produced, five of six evidence bridges were refused at their source end and the sixth could not even be identified, the numerical re-derivation confirmed the Phase 6 no-pass, and one of three numerical controls was never executed because the shared execution machinery failed ten times on the packets queued ahead of it and the maker stopped the ladder. The phase closed by an explicit one-time exception. No validation claim followed.

Two different green lights

A scientific gate asks something about nature: did the model match the registered evidence within the registered limits? A closure gate asks something about the record: did every required branch of the work finish with one of the outcomes it was allowed to have, are the artifacts present and unaltered, and does the summary claim no more than they support?

Those are different questions and can have different answers. Phase 10’s closure software re-read the pinned terminal artifacts (the final saved output files, each pinned by its fingerprint) and found a complete, internally consistent, entirely negative record. It returned green. The scientific comparison the phase had hoped to enable never ran, so there was no scientific light at all, red or green. The charter had written the case in advance: “A C0V failure or refusal or a B source refusal completes this package when reported under these rules; it blocks dependent future work but does not become a pass.” (docs/plans/phase-10-evidence-verification-execution.md, Done-when, and the charter’s Phase 10 clause.)

What Phase 10 set out to do

After Chapter 32’s all-no-pass screen, the maker was offered several packages of work and chose a bounded one, adopted on 2026-08-21. Stripped of its code names, it had five parts. A scope overlay would go through all 18 Phase 8A entries and 51 Phase 8B records and say, for each, whether the phenomenon it describes is even inside the model’s class (one vapour-grown single crystal on a fixed lattice) and what is missing before it could be compared. An intake would give terminal dispositions to 14 source payloads (24 files) the project already held but had never formally classified. Six observation bridges, B1a through B5, would each try to connect one laboratory record to one model output, operand by operand. A re-derivation, C0, would recompute the Phase 6 numerical ladder’s verdict from its stored rows without running the solver. And three absolute controls, C0V, would check the solver’s arithmetic against independently derived references.

Notice what is absent: any new habit row, any target score, any change to the physics. The charter says so: no “C1–C5 habit row, target score, D/E/F/G/H execution, external provider contact, or automatic fan-out is authorized”. In plain words: no new habit runs, no scores, none of the other lettered packages, no emails to authors, and no automatically spawned follow-up work. Phase 10 was verification, not exploration.

The dates matter for what follows. The reference derivations were published on 2026-08-22. All eleven harness runs happened on 2026-08-24. The acquisition round ran at 05:22 UTC on 2026-08-25, the six branch results were produced at 14:58 the same day, and the package closed that day. The source refusals were the last thing decided, not the first.

The bridge stopped at its source end

A bridge here is a comparison made explicit. Before any run, every operand had to be identified: the experimental quantity, its calibration and units, the matching environmental conditions, the transport boundary (a window, a wire, a falling crystal), and the model-side definition. The charter’s rule was that “a missing source, mapping, independent reference, or required state yields a named refusal or no-decision, not an invented operand”.

The bridges needed six papers or archives the project did not hold. One acquisition round was allowed. It made six requests in under four seconds and got five refusals: the 2026 Harrington–Pokrifka article, the 2025 methods paper behind the −50 °C histories, Keller and Hallett’s 1982 ventilation paper, the Princeton edition of Libbrecht’s book, and Zhao and colleagues’ 2026 article on growth in nitrogen. Each returned a paywall or an access page. The sixth request fetched a nine-megabyte supplementary video, which was stored, hashed, and never opened, because without its article there was no scale bar and no calibration to read it with.

One branch, B1b, needed nothing from outside at all: every file it required was already on the project’s own private storage. That storage failed its availability check on the intake day, 2026-08-21, so B1b’s seven operands were refused for exactly the reason Chapter 31 warned about. Where the bytes live matters as much as whether they exist.

Forty-eight missing pieces across six branches. Five branches closed as terminal refusals and one, B3, as a terminal non-identification; the phase’s formal completion rests on the refusals, because a source refusal, reported under the rules, is an allowed ending. Notice also the 21 targeted searches that were registered and never run. Each would have been allowed at most three services, four queries and 25 candidates inside a frozen date window. As far as the record shows, every branch rule required every operand, the acquisition round had already failed, and the branch protocols were frozen and run about nine hours later without any search being dispatched; a searcher that might have found an open preprint of one of those papers never ran.

Careful

“The data do not exist.” They do; the project could not get at them within its rules, which forbid paying and contacting authors, and allowed only one acquisition round plus one bounded targeted search per missing operand, searches that were registered but never run. It is fair to guess that a human with a library card could resolve several of these operands in an afternoon; that is this course’s opinion, not the record’s. The refusal is a statement about the project’s reach, not about the world.

Re-deriving the ladder without running it

Chapter 30 told you the Phase 6 numerical ladder returned NO-PASS. C0 asked a narrower question: if you throw away the original verdict and recompute it from the 80 stored rows alone, do you get the same answer? This is what Chapters 27 and 28 called an independent verifier, a second, separately written program that re-derives the verdict from the stored bytes; the Phase 10 record calls it a sibling verifier. The retained first attempt’s worker took 0.652 seconds (its publication then failed closed on a parser defect, and the published rerun’s time is not recorded). The answer was the same: 64 registered comparisons, 36 passed, 28 failed the rule that attached-cell counts must agree within half a percent. Both grid spacings failed. The registered verdict was criterion-no-pass.

The re-derivation also ranked the sources of disagreement, which the original report had not done, and the ranking is the useful physics in this phase. Changing the seed radius by one cell moved the attached count by up to 9.3 %. Halving the time step moved it by up to 3.1 %. Enlarging the box moved it by up to 4.8 % at the coarse spacing and 1.1 % at the fine one. Tightening the relaxation tolerance tenfold moved it by exactly zero, in all eight rows. The solver’s inner loop was not the problem; the observable was.

How we know

The five percentages are the largest relative attached-count difference per error source in the C0 analysis’s numerical ranking; the failure counts are the comparison rows whose difference exceeds the 0.005 tolerance copied from the Phase 6 thresholds (evidence/phase10-numerical-verification-v1/c0-comparisons.jsonl). The 48 unresolved operands are 4 + 7 + 10 + 15 + 4 + 8 from the bridges report. The suite times and the 0.148 governed process-hours are the S6 figures in docs/plans/phase-10-evidence-verification-execution.md.

Why not say “more than half the comparisons passed”? Because the criterion was registered as every comparison, and a gate is not a vote. If each row guards a required condition, one failed row blocks the claim. Averaging the rows after seeing them would replace the rule with a friendlier one. C0 in fact caught an older script that had done something similar by accident, requiring only some spacing to pass instead of every spacing; it recorded the defect as a historical limit without changing the verdict, since both spacings had failed anyway.

Still unsettled

Attached-cell count is a proxy for mass. The habit label, the thing the Nakaya comparison is about, read “neutral” on both sides of all 64 comparisons, which the record says is too coarse to count as a habit observable at all; the aspect ratio itself moved by up to 5 % in some seed and domain steps while the count drifted by percent. So the ladder never produced a shape that any count criterion could have tested. Whether the half-percent count criterion was ever the right adequacy test for a habit claim is a question the project has raised and not settled.

Three controls, three different endings

The absolute controls were meant to compare the solver’s arithmetic against answers derived independently, by hand or by a separately written program. Three were registered.

  • The radial control set up a sphere with a fixed attachment coefficient and solved the vapour field around it in closed form (an exact formula rather than a step-by-step numerical answer), two different ways. The two derivations agreed to two parts in a thousand trillion. That is agreement between two hand derivations of a textbook problem; it says nothing yet about the solver, and the artifact forbids reading it as physical realism or validation. The reference was frozen and pinned. The packets were queued preflight first, then the moving control, then radial, and the ladder never reached the third, for the reason the next section explains.
  • The moving control set up a tiny crystal whose first growth event could be timed exactly, then checked a reference derivation against an independent one. The two derivations agreed on every numeric quantity to sixteen decimal places: the two residuals were about 1.2 and 1.8 parts in ten quadrillion, against a frozen tolerance of one part in a hundred trillion. But one condition in the checker’s record, an exact match of the endpoint record, came out false, and the pre-registered rule said any disagreement between reference and check closes the layer as a refusal. It closed. You may judge whether that was an honest refusal or a pedantic one; the answer key is that a rule registered before the result is not renegotiated after it.
  • The static control asked for an independent reference for the full three-dimensional field. The current solver contract offered none that did not share the solver’s own arithmetic, and comparing the solver to itself at finer grids was explicitly forbidden as a substitute. It was refused before implementation.

When the checking machine became the experiment

To run the radial and moving controls, the project built an execution harness. It had a preflight, the check that runs before anything is allowed to start, which froze every registered output, its producer (the program that writes it) and its check. It had a parent process that locked, launched and audited a worker, the child program that does the calculation. It had a watchdog, a timer that kills a run that hangs; canonical hashing of every artifact, which fingerprints a file after formatting it one standard way so that the same content always gives the same fingerprint; and a recovery procedure for when any of that failed. On 2026-08-24 the harness was run eleven times. Ten runs stopped themselves before reaching the science. Every stop was a defect in the harness, and every stop was also the harness doing what fail-closed design requires. The problem was not that it checked; it was how much of the phase the checking consumed.

Read the causes. A version string compared “v24.13.1” with “Node v24.13.1”. Windows handed the child process nine ordinary environment variables the frozen roster did not list. A file path pointed at version two when the run was version three. Five hand-typed labels in the executor still said version one when the moving run was version two. A digest hashed pretty-printed JSON on one side and compact JSON on the other. None of these is physics. None of them is even the solver. Each one cost a new frozen recovery version, a full test suite of twenty to thirty minutes, and an audit that reported zero blockers and then missed the seam the next run hit. Five of the ten stops (runs 3, 4, 9, 10 and 11) trace to one repeated mistake: code that read the frozen version-2 paths of the immutable matrix where the live version-3 overlay was needed. The package had a budget of 24 governed process-hours for its controls and used 0.148 of them, nearly all on the preflight checking its own obligations. Time the harness spent policing itself and time spent on the experiment are different columns, and the second column stayed at zero.

The maker stopped it on the tenth failure. The decision record that closed the phase says why in one clause: the “process had become disproportionate to the solo research decision”. It closed the package by a one-time exception, and it says, in the same breath, what the exception is not: “This is a one-package exception, not a rule that crashes become scientific refusals or that authorized experiments may be skipped.”

Key idea

Checking is not free, and past a point it is not even neutral. Every layer of assurance you add is code that can fail, and when it fails it consumes the time the experiment needed. Phase 10 spent more than five hours of timed test suites and nine recovery cycles to run zero seconds of solver. The green closure light is honest about that: it certifies the record, not the science, and not the wisdom of the process.

The tripwire already existed. On 2026-08-12, after Phase 8B’s own overspend (Chapter 31), the working rules gained one: “if process consumes roughly one quarter of a work block without producing source coverage, measurements, calculations, code, experiments, or the requested decision, or if a second meta-validation layer appears, stop and simplify before continuing.” Phase 10 ran past it anyway. On 2026-08-24, the day of the eleven runs, the project added two sub-rules with the corrective: “The corrective action is simplification and delivery, not another process document, gate, audit round, or metric for tracking the excess.” (Both from AGENTS.md, Rule 14.) Chapter 29 warned that this project had not found the knee of the rigour curve. Phase 10 is where it went over a line it had already drawn, noticed, and sharpened the rule.

Stamp the exact outcome

“Refused”, “incomplete”, “not executed” and “no verdict” are four different words, and the closure record uses each one deliberately. A refusal is a decision reached under a rule. Incomplete means the work was stopped by a decision, not finished. Not executed means the machinery never reached the science. No verdict means nobody is allowed to say pass or fail. Compressing them into one vague failure would destroy information the next investigator needs about what authority would be required to continue.

What the project earned

Phase 10 did not earn a better crystal model. It earned a reproducible stopping point: the forty-eight missing operands are named, the numerical no-pass is preserved with its causes ranked, the abandoned routes keep their exact status, the verifier is written to reject a falsely upgraded report, and the rulebook now contains a sharpened rule against the failure mode that consumed it. That is less than the project hoped for and more than most projects write down.

It is also the larger lesson of this course. Computer methods are not only ways to calculate faster. Used carefully, they protect the boundary between what we hoped, what we tried, what the evidence showed, and what remains unknown. Used carelessly, in either direction, they blur it: too little checking lets a wish become a claim; too much lets the checking become the work.

What is happening now

This course follows the project’s chartered phases, and the last of those closed on 2026-08-25. The project did not stop. Since then the maker has been running exploratory campaigns on the same solver: hundreds of growth runs mapping how the model’s shape depends on temperature, forcing, air pressure and the starting seed, comparing the M1 kinetics with its no-dip ablation from Chapter 30. The work lives on its own branch (explore/post-phase10-discovery) under two plans, docs/plans/post-phase10-discovery-campaign.md and docs/plans/post-phase10-adaptive-discovery.md, whose stated claim level is “exploratory model-development evidence only”: a first campaign analysed on 2026-08-28, a 432-row tranche and a long wave completed since, and a 58-row confirmation wave launched on 2026-09-02. It has no charter gate, no registered comparison and no validation claim, and this course does not teach its results. If it ever passes through a chartered gate, it will earn a chapter of its own.

Part Two asked whether a program that was given the right physics could be shown to reproduce reality, and the honest answer, after ten phases, is: not yet, and here is exactly why not.
