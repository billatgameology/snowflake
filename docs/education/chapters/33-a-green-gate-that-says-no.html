Chapter 33

A Green Gate That Says No

Software can correctly verify that a scientific attempt did not pass. The green check belongs to the honesty of the record, not to the physics.

Guiding questionHow can a phase finish successfully when the proposed scientific bridge is refused?

By the end, you can

  • Separate a closure-gate pass from a scientific comparison pass.
  • Explain what an unresolved operand does to a proposed evidence bridge.
  • Read the Phase 10 numerical ladder without averaging away failed rows.
  • Describe what Phase 10 did not authorize.
Final project boundary through Phase 10: Phase 10 is complete-negative. Its verifier passed 7/7 closure checks, but no target, habit, C1–C5, or production radial comparison row ran. No solver change, validation claim, or downstream authorization resulted.

There are two different green lights

A scientific gate asks something about nature: did the model match the registered evidence within the registered limits? A closure gate asks something about the project record: did every required branch finish with an allowed, evidence-backed outcome?

Those questions can have different answers. Phase 10’s closure software found a complete and internally consistent negative record. It therefore returned green. The scientific comparison it hoped to enable was not completed, so there was no scientific green light.

The bridge stopped at its source end

Phase 10 tried to define a narrow bridge from historical evidence to a model comparison. Before running a simulator, the project had to identify every needed operand: the experimental quantity, its calibration and units, the matching environmental conditions, and the model-side definition.

The source review ended in a registered terminal refusal. Across six branches, 48 required operands remained unresolved. A later worker could not repair those gaps from the governed sources without guessing or changing the question. That made the intended bridge unfit for execution.

The numerical ladder also said no

A separate C0 numerical ladder checked whether the existing computation behaved acceptably across a registered set of comparisons. Of 64 comparisons, 36 passed and 28 failed an attached-count condition. The correct verdict was criterion-no-pass.

Why not say “more than half passed”? Because a gate is not a popularity contest. If every row protects a required condition, one failed required row can block the claim. Averaging the rows would quietly replace the registered rule after seeing the answer.

Other paths stayed visibly unfinished or unexecuted

The maker terminated the C0V branch; it remains incomplete and no-pass. A radial reference was frozen, but its production comparison was not executed, so it has no verdict. Moving-reference work closed by reference-discrepancy refusal. A static route closed before implementation.

These labels are deliberately different. “Refused,” “incomplete,” “not executed,” and “no verdict” should not be compressed into one vague failure. Each tells the next investigator what happened and what authority would be needed to continue.

What the project earned

Phase 10 did not earn a better crystal model. It earned a reproducible stopping point: the source gaps are named, the numerical no-pass is preserved, abandoned routes keep their exact status, and the verifier can reject a falsely upgraded report.

That is the larger lesson of this course. Computer methods are not only ways to calculate faster. Used carefully, they can protect the boundary between what we hoped, what we tried, what the evidence showed, and what remains unknown.
