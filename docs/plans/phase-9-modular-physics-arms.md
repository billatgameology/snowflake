# Plan — Phase 9: protocol-matched modular physics experiments

- **Phase:** Phase 9 (proposed) — explain measured behaviour with separable physics arms
- **Status:** evidence-level draft for maker review; not chartered and not authorized for execution
- **Phase 8B role:** the successor corpus's Phase 9 impact report and proposed consumer plan
- **Started:** 2026-08-04
- **Last touched:** 2026-08-12 by OpenAI Codex after the Phase 9 knowledge-baseline review

## Goal

Use the Phase 8B benchmark to find which missing or mis-specified mechanisms explain measured
growth behaviour under source-matched conditions. Phase 9 factors candidate physics into separately
switchable arms, starts with the cheapest measurement that can distinguish each arm, and promotes a
module only when its pre-registered effect survives that confrontation.

Phase 8B materially changes the original draft. The usable foundation is not one morphology label
or one claimed pressure ratio. It is 16 native levitated mass histories, two native substrate-grown
dimension histories, direct substrate and free-fall trajectory series, low-pressure basal/prism
facet-rate series, and controlled thermal-conductivity/vapour-diffusivity interventions. Every one
of those measurements is model-development evidence because it shaped this plan. The corrected
residual audit also adds Bacon–Baker–Swanson's directly reported initiation/aspect summary and
mass-growth-factor contrast. None may later be relabeled held out or used to claim quantitative
validation.

The [Phase 9 knowledge baseline](../phase9-knowledge-guide.md) adds an 18-source / 15-hypothesis
theory map. It changes this draft in two material ways: a learned mass-conditioned transfer law is
cheap enough to test as a scalar pre-solver discriminator, and step-source location is a distinct
hollowing mechanism that must be tested against, not silently absorbed into, width-conditioned
SDAK.

## Proposed done when

The charter has no Phase 9 milestone. If adopted, Phase 9 is done when every registered arm has:

1. an accepted ADR and frozen source-to-model condition mapping;
2. an expected effect and a no-effect/failure branch committed before a solver run;
3. a cheapest-discriminator result reported against exact Phase 8B rows, including disagreement;
4. an ablation showing what changed relative to the same baseline; and
5. for any promoted campaign arm, a frozen protocol, protocol-compatibility gate, negative control,
   numerical-control result, and row-derived score over its declared development domain.

It is a valid outcome for every arm to fail. No Phase 8B comparison grants a validation label.

## Evidence boundary inherited from Phase 8B

- All 51 selected P0/P1/P2 records are model development. The successor split contains no held-out
  row. A future confirmatory comparison needs genuinely unused evidence and a separately frozen
  split before its values are inspected.
- Raw observations remain separate from coalescing, interpolation, derivatives, fitted exponents,
  and loss functions. Phase 9 must register each downstream operator before using it to rank arms.
- A benchmark is scoreable only if the solver run represents, or explicitly bounds, its source
  geometry, support, transport, forcing semantics, and observable. Substrate-grown, free-fall, and
  electrodynamically levitated specimens are not interchangeable.
- The two −50 °C dimension histories may be used as dimension-versus-time and change-point-response
  development data. Their source labels `48%` and `20%` may not be converted to solver
  `sigmaInfinity` until the first-report Methods establish the supersaturation reference and
  uncertainty.
- Shared raw data, campaign, investigator, or calibration lineage counts once. Multiple papers or
  views of one specimen do not create independent agreement.

## Approach

Use a protocol-compatibility gate before a numerical score, then run one-factor arms before
combinations. The permanent `GGThreshold` and existing LibbrechtKinetics arms remain controls.
Historical Phase 6 outputs are never rewritten; Phase 9 creates new protocols and evidence.

### Measurement adapters and scoring

Phase 9 needs five explicit adapters before a common score exists:

- planar-facet normal velocity versus surface or far-field supersaturation, with the source's
  diffusion correction and supersaturation semantics stated;
- supported-crystal radius/thickness or height/radius histories, with substrate and asymmetric
  transport either represented or declared a transfer limitation;
- free-particle mass-ratio histories, with the source's likely-polycrystalline seed status retained;
- free-fall mean dimensions and empirical order spans, with ventilation and ensemble semantics
  represented; and
- initiation-state aggregates, preserving the reported aspect intervals, sparse central seed band,
  initial-size difference, and unequal mass-growth factors without inventing per-particle rows or a
  population denominator.

Each adapter first publishes aligned source/model rows and uncertainty components. Loss functions,
interpolation grids, derivative windows, and any family weighting are then frozen without looking at
arm rankings. Report per-series residuals and signs as well as any aggregate; a large family may not
silently drown out a five-point intervention. Bacon's aggregate aspect intervals are a bounded
M-S discriminator, not permission to turn legacy habit labels into a universal score.

D-BT uses the free-particle adapter but is intentionally outside the 3-D solver. It first performs
an experiment-ID overlap join against Lamb et al.'s cited training datasets, then forward-integrates
each native history separately. Primary scoring is restricted to 0–500 s for the six histories
inside 205–240 K. In each leave-one-history-out fold, one global constant `Gc` multiplier is fit on
the other five histories and evaluated on the held-out history beside continuum, Nelson–Baker and
the learned law. Hotter conditions and later rows are extrapolation. A pooled point-weighted loss
is forbidden because it would let the longest trace decide the result.

M-V is an eligibility gate for the free-fall adapter, not a later explanatory flourish. No M-PT
absolute score is published until either the quantitative velocity source is represented or a
documented bound identifies a ventilation-negligible subset.

M-W and M-SS share a spatial/rim adapter but not a mechanism. M-W changes the material barrier as a
function of physical facet width. M-SS changes the source and flux closure. Final aspect ratio alone
is ineligible to rank them because ordinary diffusion gradients, substrate asymmetry and either
mechanism can produce similar endpoints.
The Harrington–Pokrifka histories are same-lineage source-replay evidence for M-SS; they cannot
independently confirm its cause. Causal promotion requires an independently triggered hollowing
transition.

## Proposed work sequence

- [ ] **S0 — Adopt and freeze the research contract.** Maker-approved ADR plus matching charter
  amendment; bind the Phase 8B successor manifest, all-development split, compute envelope, module
  roster, no-pass branch, and new-evidence policy.
- [ ] **S1 — Build protocol-compatibility and measurement adapters.** Implement the five adapters
  above with fixture tests and explicit ineligible outcomes. No module ranking yet.
- [ ] **S2 — Re-score permanent controls.** Run `GGThreshold` and LibbrechtKinetics only where an
  adapter says the protocol is representable. This establishes the measured mismatch vector; it
  does not change historical Phase 6 evidence.
- [ ] **S3 — D-BT scalar replay.** Perform the training-ID overlap join, then run the frozen
  leave-one-history-out comparison over the six in-domain 0–500 s histories. Report hotter and
  later rows only as extrapolation. Do not change a solver or infer a facet mechanism from a
  bulk-law win.
- [ ] **S4 — M-F planar-facet probe.** Confront basal and prism rate curves at all three measured
  temperatures. Halt morphology interpretation if the surface operator misses the direct local
  observable.
- [ ] **S5 — M-V compatibility decision.** Acquire the full controlled-velocity source or derive a
  documented eligibility bound. Mark ineligible free-fall rows before any M-PT absolute score.
- [ ] **S6 — M-PT orthogonal-intervention probe.** For the eligible subset, separate the observed
  vapour- and heat-transport responses against the two controlled Gonda–Komabayasi one-factor
  families; do not infer an interaction without a crossed experiment.
- [ ] **S7 — M-PK one-bar replay and evidence decision.** Compare transport-only and the source-
  derived pressure-conditioned rule against the one-bar −5 °C trajectories under one frozen
  mapping. Do not call this pressure identification; acquire a cross-pressure trajectory before
  promoting the arm.
- [ ] **S8 — M-H path and memory split.** First compare exact-path and endpoint-only runs only as a
  forcing-fidelity test. Test memory only by comparing exact-path Markov and named stateful models
  on matched-endpoint or post-event relaxation rows; otherwise report memory non-identifiable.
- [ ] **S9 — M-W versus M-SS hollowing probes.** Compare the newer Libbrecht edition before freezing
  monograph-only M-W inputs, freeze the physical width definition, and
  acquire the full Harrington–Pokrifka 2026 equation set before implementing M-SS. Compare spatial
  growth or rim-width trajectories under the same transport and forcing adapter. Treat the
  companion histories as source replay and require independent trigger evidence for causal
  promotion; do not select by final shape.
- [ ] **S10 — M-S, M-LH, and M-GT bounded probes.** For M-S, first reproduce the unequal initial-size
  and mass-exposure protocols without a seed-state mechanism, then add one named initiation-state
  intervention; report only interval/range direction unless new matched evidence is frozen. Keep
  M-LH direction-only until the exact-condition resistance is calculated; run M-GT only as a
  curvature/numerical diagnostic.
- [ ] **S11 — M-K2 annex and one-factor promotions.** Transcribe and hash the annex, test it against
  direct development rows, then promote only arms that met their pre-committed effects.
- [ ] **S12 — Combination campaign or measured no-pass.** Combine only individually supported
  modules, freeze interaction hypotheses and negative controls, and publish agreements,
  disagreements, incompatibilities, and numerical limits.

## Out of scope

- Held-out validation or a quantitative-validation claim from any Phase 8B row.
- Selecting modules, thresholds, interpolation rules, or weights by the best campaign score.
- Treating fitted attachment inputs as independent observations.
- Quantitative polycrystal, nucleation-population, substrate, or ventilation claims without an
  implemented source-matched protocol.
- GPU execution or changes to Phase 6/7 artifacts.
- A full thermal fluid solve unless a later ADR expands the proposed phase.

## Tried and rejected

- **Treat the Takahashi cross-study +30% mass difference as a clean `D(P)` target.** Rejected:
  liquid-water content, apparatus, population, ventilation, temperature drift, and riming are
  confounded. Direct Gonda–Komabayasi interventions and Libbrecht's pressure correction are the
  defensible development evidence.
- **Make Gibbs–Thomson the second module because it is printed physics.** Rejected as a scientific
  priority: Phase 8B found no matched direct experiment. Keep it as a numerical diagnostic until a
  measurement exists.
- **Call ventilation optional while scoring free-fall targets.** Rejected: controlled velocity can
  change habit, so ventilation is a protocol gate even if the eventual decision is to restrict the
  target instead of implementing a model.
- **Preserve the Phase 8A held-out labels after using the measurements to design Phase 9.** Rejected
  by decision 0048's leakage rule. The successor corpus is all model development.
- **Score every source through one scalar immediately.** Rejected: facet rates, supported
  dimensions, levitated mass, and free-fall ensemble means require different protocol adapters and
  uncertainty semantics.
- **Digitize Bacon Figures 6–7 merely because their labels are readable.** Rejected for the current
  M-S probe: the seed and droplet particles differ in initial size and mass-growth factor, the source
  gives no population denominator, and the printed Table 1 plus mass-growth aggregates already
  support the bounded intervention design. Per-particle digitization becomes useful only if a later
  adopted protocol can represent those confounds and needs a numeric distributional score.
- **Install Lamb's learned bulk law as a local attachment rule.** Rejected: it predicts one
  particle-level transfer coefficient from mass and continuum transport and has no facet,
  morphology, support, ventilation or initiation state. It is useful as D-BT, not M-F.
- **Treat all hollowing as width-conditioned SDAK.** Rejected: self-consistent flux and step-source
  location predict a competing spatial mechanism. M-W and M-SS need the same observations and must
  be compared explicitly.
- **Call endpoint-only versus exact-path improvement a memory result.** Rejected: any memoryless
  dynamic model benefits from receiving the correct forcing. Memory needs matched resolved states
  or a post-event relaxation discriminator.
- **Identify pressure dependence from the selected one-bar trajectories.** Rejected: they can
  replay a source-derived rule but contain no pressure intervention. A cross-pressure trajectory is
  required for promotion.

  left source-blocked and unimplemented.

- Which cross-pressure trajectory, if any, is frozen before M-PK can be promoted beyond source

- Whether the newer Libbrecht monograph edition changes any monograph-only M-W input.

  split; none of the Phase 8B benchmark qualifies.
