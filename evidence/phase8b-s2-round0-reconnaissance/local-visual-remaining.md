### Model variables, conditions, and limitations

- The source defines a mesoscopic stacked-triangular lattice with eight neighbors, two mass fields, and a four-step update. Vapor density, vertical drift, seven freezing fractions, seven melting rates, and seven geometry-indexed attachment thresholds are tunable. The paper's printed “20-site” canonical radius-2 seed is a miscount; the sixfold-symmetric lattice hexagon has 19 sites.
- A reduced four-knob search uses vapor density, a convexifying threshold, a smoothing/melting rate, and a directional threshold ratio. Parameters are not mapped a priori to temperature, pressure, or physical supersaturation. The source calls realistic selection guesswork, uses ad hoc rules, and says the best examples require judicious tuning and may be highly perturbation- and seed-sensitive.
- Case selection is by visual resemblance to snow-crystal photographs. There is no reported metric, uncertainty interval, blind selection, ensemble frequency, or source-matched condition table.
- Omitted or approximate physics include latent-heat diffusion, surface diffusion, surface impedance, molecular-scale lattice detail, and a rigorously established continuum limit. The finite reflecting reservoir lets vapor density fall during growth, unlike a maintained far-field laboratory condition. Published 3-D examples are deterministic; the randomized rule is only proposed.

**Phase 9 relevance.** This is the primary source for computational machinery and permanent differential-control behavior, not a quantitative target corpus. Its most valuable Phase 9 uses are bitwise replay, schedule/seed/parameter-ablation tests, and guardrails against claiming visual similarity as physical validation. Its figures cannot be counted as independent laboratory witnesses or used to tune and score the same G-G rule.

### Protocol, direct observables, lineage, and uncertainty

- Each record is one selected particle grown without a support substrate in the Penn State Button Electrode Levitation thermal-gradient diffusion chamber, near atmospheric pressure (about 970 hPa). A charged water droplet is optically sized while liquid, frozen, held by electrode feedback, and followed longitudinally.
- **Direct instrument-level growth observable:** normalized levitation voltage, interpreted as mass ratio `m/m0(t)`. The paper assigns a maximum 5% relative error to mass ratio. Raw sampling is 10–30 Hz. Initial droplet radius is inferred by matching scattering/diffraction to Mie theory, typically to about 1 micrometre; it is not a direct geometric image measurement.
- Ambient chamber ice supersaturation is model/calibration-derived, not directly measured at the surface. Plate temperature uncertainty is ±0.2 C and chamber depth ±0.1 mm, typically yielding about 10% relative supersaturation uncertainty; Table 1 prints row-specific uncertainties. Pressure uncertainty and covariance among condition fields are absent.
- The heterogeneous family reuses Harrison et al. 2016 Snomax-frozen-droplet raw traces. This paper corrects conditions and reinterprets those same records; it is **one witness, not another campaign**. The prose analyzes 17 HPLC-water homogeneous-freezing experiments, but Table 1 visibly prints 18 homogeneous condition rows; the manuscript does not identify an excluded row. That denominator/join discrepancy must be resolved before row-level use. Later work qualifies these frozen-droplet products as likely polycrystalline; this manuscript did not image their shapes.
- Shape, facet orientation, grain count, linear dimensions, and habit were not observed during growth. Spherical/isometric shape, faceted-growth mechanism, and kinetics-transition labels are model assumptions or interpretations.

### Other numeric prose and complete scope disposition

- The apparatus, vapor-growth equations, scaled-rate transform, and power-law derivation occupy pp. 7–15. They define analysis operators, not additional measurements. The main transform removes direct dependence on ambient supersaturation but remains spherical/isometric and constant-condition dependent; the paper warns it cannot alone prove diffusion-limited growth.
- Results/discussion on pp. 16–27 reuse Table 1 and Figs. 5–15. Ten of 17 homogeneous cases fit constant-mechanism faceted growth; seven require the empirical transition surrogate. Revised fitted ranges are 0.008–1 for heterogeneous cases and 0.004–1 for constant-mechanism homogeneous cases; transition fits fall below 0.002. All are inversions/partitions, not direct observations.
- The appendix on pp. 28–30 and Fig. 16 revise the chamber calibration, especially at low supersaturation. This is why Harrison 2016 and this paper must be joined as one lineage with corrected conditions, not compared as independent laboratories.
- References and lists on pp. 31–40 contain no additional measurements beyond the indexed table/figure locators.

**Phase 9 relevance.** The strongest eventual target family is the direct `m/m0(t)` lineage, with corrected Table 1 conditions and exact native-member joins. The heterogeneous traces are suitable only as one Penn State BEL witness; the homogeneous curves require plot extraction unless their native dataset is later acquired. Supersaturation-free scaled-rate and power-law diagnostics are promising derived confrontations but require preregistered smoothing/derivative/uncertainty operators. Fitted attachment coefficients, characteristic supersaturation, transition labels, and assumed shapes are development/mechanism evidence and must not be used to validate the same kinetics model. Polycrystal, nucleation-mode, and unobserved-shape physics are presently outside the solver and must remain explicit.
