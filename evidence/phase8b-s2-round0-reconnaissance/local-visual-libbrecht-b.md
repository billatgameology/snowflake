# Phase 8B S2 page-complete visual audit — four later Libbrecht sources

This is page-complete S2 reconnaissance, not either formal S3 independent classification pass.
All 76 PDF pages were inspected visually offline from the NAS page renders, with the source PDFs
and existing tracked extracts used as reading aids. No target coordinates were digitized and no
new source was requested from the network. The purpose of this record is to separate direct
observations from reused literature, derived quantities, model outputs and explanatory sketches
before Phase 9 chooses confrontation targets.

## Libbrecht 2021 — triangular snowflakes

Source: `2106.09809v1.pdf`, *Triangular Snowflakes: Growing Structures with Three-fold
Symmetry using a Hexagonal Ice Crystal Lattice*, Kenneth G. Libbrecht, arXiv `2106.09809`,
19 PDF pages, SHA-256
`f53b9e64a1a6f149a06aba93474041335d8df0653c2615a1b8c90b6c8b1aaa87`.
Scope verdict: **include the controlled -14 °C needle-seed observations and their morphology
metrics; retain natural and earlier-study images as separate lineages**.

Potential measurement units and exclusions:

- PDF p1, Fig. 1: two laboratory-grown triangular plates on c-axis columns. Direct morphology
  examples, but the caption supplies no run-level condition or time beyond the source-wide
  experimental framing.
- PDF p2, Fig. 2: Scoresby's 1820 sketch; secondary historical evidence only. PDF p2, Fig. 3:
  four natural crystals photographed by the author, with three plate sizes spanning 0.6--1.4 mm
  and one branched crystal about 2.5 mm tip-to-tip. These are primary field images but have no
  controlled temperature, supersaturation or growth history and are not the chamber lineage.
- PDF p3, Fig. 4 and PDF p4, Fig. 5: the earlier -10 °C free-fall data from `[2009Lib4]` expressed
  in the paper's hexagonality/triangularity plane and twelve example images. Fig. 4 excludes
  crystals above the stated hexagonality cutoff; it is a re-presentation of an earlier ensemble,
  not a new independent experiment. The plotted points and image identities should remain linked.
- PDF pp5--6, Figs. 6--7: direct apparatus/seed examples from the dual diffusion chamber at one
  atmosphere. Needles were made at -6 °C under high electric field, grew to 1--2 mm, then were
  transferred without the field; displayed plate diameters are roughly 200 and 400 micrometres.
- PDF p7, Fig. 8: the strongest quantitative unit in this source. One complete -14 °C run slowly
  lowered far-field supersaturation over several hours while new needle seeds were introduced.
  The graph records morphology metric versus supersaturation for every reasonably well-formed
  crystal, while a few explicitly malformed outliers were omitted. It shows hexagonal plates above
  about 8%, a narrow high-yield trigonal regime below that, and blocky/columnar forms below about
  6%. Additional runs are said to reproduce the trend but are not shown. Extract the plotted points,
  the exclusion rule and run-level lineage together.
- PDF p7, Fig. 9: condition-linked example plates annotated with measured triangularity. These are
  image witnesses for the Fig. 8 regime, not an independent ensemble.
- PDF p8, Figs. 10--11: two time-image series. Fig. 10 follows one near-equilateral plate whose
  sharp tips formed when its size was 20 micrometres or less. Fig. 11 follows a two-tip plate for
  33 minutes to a longest facet of 320 micrometres. Images support event/time and morphology
  coding; the source does not publish complete coordinate histories.
- PDF p9, Fig. 12: geometric explanatory sketch only. The derived two-to-one tip/facet velocity
  requirement is an analytic consequence of the assumed 60-degree geometry, not a measured
  velocity series.
- PDF p10, Fig. 13: one perturbation pair. A plate with 165-micrometre facets is shown before and
  35 minutes after lowering supersaturation, with loss of sharp triangular tips. Direct
  image/event evidence; exact before/after supersaturations are not printed in the caption.
- PDF p11, Fig. 14: one supersaturation perturbation sequence, from about 7% to about 12%, showing
  two sharp tips broadening and ridge/splitting development. Preserve the three images as one run.
- PDF p12, Fig. 15: one trigonal-plate before/after sequence. Facet-motion measurements imply the
  short facets initially moved about 60% faster than the long facets; after supersaturation was
  raised, the ridge traces indicate nearly equal rates. This is the source's only explicit measured
  facet-velocity ratio, but the later prose says substantially more careful measurements would be
  required for a quantitative conclusion.
- PDF p14, Fig. 16: observed prevalence comparison of triangular versus absent/rare diamond-like
  forms, illustrated by an image and drawing. No sample count is published, so retain as qualitative
  prose evidence only. PDF pp14--16, Figs. 17--18: formation sketches and a speculative mechanism;
  exclude from the measured-data corpus while retaining the hypotheses they connect to the images.

The controlled contribution is therefore one plotted morphology/supersaturation run plus a small
set of linked image sequences and one approximate facet-velocity ratio, all restricted to
one-atmosphere air, -14 °C and c-axis needle seeds. The source gives no run-level statistical
uncertainty and explicitly limits the proposed mechanism to these conditions. It is useful to
Phase 9 for schedule/history and three-fold-symmetry behavior, not as a plate-seed endpoint target.

## Libbrecht 2021 — taxonomy using c-axis needle seeds

Source: `2109.00098v1.pdf`, *A Taxonomy of Snow Crystal Growth Behaviors: 1. Using c-axis
Ice Needles as Seed Crystals*, Kenneth G. Libbrecht, arXiv `2109.00098`, 23 PDF pages,
SHA-256 `e382edbc61e706c4cdb88811bba2488f7d29baf8dd14d94b21e4a12f5d3fbbeb`.
Scope verdict: **include the apparatus calibration and needle-seed observation matrix as
qualitative primary evidence; exclude the extensive historical/model review from local counts**.

Potential measurement units and exclusions:

- PDF pp2--4, Figs. 1--8: historical classification diagrams, split-star examples and published
  Nakaya diagrams. They are secondary/contextual and must not be counted as this experiment's
  observations.
- PDF pp5--7, Figs. 9--10 and 14: previously published computational crystals; model outputs only.
  Fig. 11 re-presents a prior five-image experimental sequence and a fitted two-dimensional model;
  it belongs to the cited earlier experiment and is not an independent witness here. Figs. 12--13
  are substrate/seed geometry illustrations.
- PDF p11, Fig. 15: apparatus geometry for the two connected diffusion chambers. PDF p12, Fig. 16:
  a directly observed typical vertical temperature profile, reported linear and horizontally
  stable; this is calibration evidence. PDF p12, Fig. 17: calculated chamber supersaturation field,
  hence derived rather than directly measured. The chamber height is 10 cm and observations occur
  near the centre.
- PDF p12, Fig. 18: direct seed-production image at 2000 V, with the longest electric needle about
  3 mm. PDF p13, Fig. 19: direct plate-on-needle example after transfer and field removal.
- PDF p13, Fig. 20: three illumination views of the same crystal; useful image-method evidence,
  not three specimens.
- PDF p14, Figs. 21--23: direct morphology examples from the chamber. Fig. 21 is a cup with six
  fins near (-7 °C, 32%); Fig. 22 is a conical plate with ridge/I-beam development but no exact
  condition in the caption; Fig. 23 is a fishbone dendrite near -5 °C at high supersaturation.
  Treat these as individual qualitative examples unless underlying run records are later found.
- PDF pp19--23, Fig. 24a--e: the main local data object, a five-page pictorial morphology matrix
  of needle-tip crystals in one-atmosphere air. Each panel is condition-labelled and the prose says
  each used a constant growth environment. The matrix spans warm plates, the -5 °C column/fishbone
  regime, the -8 to -10 °C block/ridge transition, very thin plates near -14 °C and colder plate/
  block/I-beam forms. Some cells are absent because warm fast growth caused melting or the linear
  chamber could not reach the requested cold/high-supersaturation condition. The author explicitly
  calls these qualitative snapshots, so image morphology and stated conditions are direct but no
  endpoint dimension or rate should be inferred from image scale alone.

This source establishes seed and apparatus semantics and a broad qualitative response surface. It
does not provide a row-wise numeric table, replicate counts or uncertainties for Fig. 24, and its
matrix cannot be treated as independent of later observations without specimen/campaign
reconciliation. For Phase 9 it is strongest as development evidence for seed-dependent morphology
families and coverage gaps, not a ready numeric scoring table.

## Libbrecht and Walkling 2023 — faceting model and archived observations

Source: `2306.04042v1.pdf`, *A Comprehensive Model of Snow Crystal Faceting*, Kenneth
Libbrecht and James Walkling, arXiv `2306.04042`, 20 PDF pages, SHA-256
`1ff2c1f9699c2aefd26e5373f29c4fdd7a110620c136bd349d813947dacbcd1f`.
Scope verdict: **include the small archived experimental image series with their printed
dimensions/rates; classify the governing curves and parameter table as derived/model evidence**.

Potential measurement units and exclusions:

- PDF p5, Fig. 1: idealized prism geometry. PDF p10, Figs. 2--3: analytic model predictions of
  faceting proxy versus growth velocity for assumed 20- and 500-micrometre crystals. Exclude the
  curves from direct-measurement targets.
- PDF p9, Table 1: exact printed prism-kinetics prescription at -1, -2, -3, -5, -7 and -15 °C,
  including kinetic velocity and two nucleation branches where applicable. The parameters were
  chosen from prior experimental measurements and become more uncertain toward 0 °C; this is a
  derived/model input table, not six new experiment rows. Preserve it exactly because no plot
  digitization is needed and the source calls the high-temperature second branch speculative.
- PDF p12, Fig. 4: representative direct images at -7 °C and 50 Torr on sapphire; each image box
  is 50 micrometres, growth about 60 s and rate about 150 nm/s. The resolution is stated
  insufficient for an accurate corner-radius ratio, so only morphology/faceting is directly usable.
- PDF p12, Fig. 5: two individual crystals at -2 °C and 20 Torr on sapphire. Printed
  `(radius, height)` values are (20, 37) and (19, 18) micrometres, with prism rates about 50 and
  100 nm/s. Laser interferometry measured the rates.
- PDF p12, Fig. 6: two individual crystals at -1 °C in near vacuum on sapphire, with printed
  `(radius, height)` values (29, 21) and (25, 24) micrometres and rates about 200 and 100 nm/s.
- PDF p13, Fig. 7: four -2 °C near-vacuum crystals on sapphire: `(radius, height)` values
  (37, 10), (23, 7), (28, 18), and (27, 18) micrometres with reported rates about 100, 75, 500,
  and 650 nm/s. The first two retain faceting while latent heating is invoked for the faster pair.
  These are separate archived examples, but initial shapes and other conditions were not carefully
  recorded, and the authors warn against overinterpreting differences among Figs. 5--7.
- PDF p13, Fig. 8: four face-on stages and one side view of a single plate-on-pedestal crystal at
  -2 °C and one bar over about 14 minutes, ending near 50-micrometre effective radius. The caption
  prints a final velocity of about 100 micrometres/s, inconsistent in scale with adjacent examples;
  preserve the printed unit and flag it for source clarification rather than silently correcting it.
- PDF p14, Fig. 9: two direct plate-on-needle observations in one-bar air at 8% far-field
  supersaturation. At -0.15 °C the radius is about 55 micrometres and rate about 85 nm/s; at
  -1 °C the radius is about 35 micrometres and rate about 70 nm/s.
- PDF p15, Fig. 10: one 2-mm disk growing in a thin film of slightly supercooled liquid on glass;
  direct qualitative observation but a different ice/liquid system, so exclude it from vapor-growth
  scoring while retaining it as interface-comparison context.

No consistent uncertainty columns or replicate counts accompany Figs. 4--9. Most are selected
archive examples with mixed apparatus, pressure, substrate and seed conditions. Their printed
dimensions, velocities and morphology can confront high-temperature faceting/thermal behavior,
but they are not a homogeneous ensemble and cannot independently validate the model fitted around
them.

## Libbrecht 2023 — quantitative taxonomy matrix

Source: `2306.13087v1.pdf`, *A Taxonomy of Snow Crystal Growth Behaviors: 2. Quantifying
the Nakaya Diagram*, Kenneth G. Libbrecht, arXiv `2306.13087`, 14 PDF pages, SHA-256
`20f579e01777d51b81b527751b32c3e44b1d8ebe9f1d09a7f15554c2445381af`.
Scope verdict: **include the 206 primary condition-labelled observation panels as one linked
campaign; keep the printed starter-model prescriptions separate as derived/in-sample material**.

Potential measurement units and exclusions:

- PDF p6, Fig. 1: temperature-dependent basal/prism nucleation-parameter curves for the M1 starter
  model. They are generated by printed, explicitly ad hoc closed forms based mainly on prior
  measurements plus morphology-informed dip placement. They are exact model prescriptions, not
  direct observations from the 206-crystal campaign.
- PDF pp11--14, Fig. 2: 206 photographic observations arranged by temperature and far-field
  supersaturation. Each panel gives a growth time and field-of-view width; the latter is not an
  endpoint crystal dimension. The rows cover stated supersaturations 7, 10, 15, 20, 30, 45, 70,
  100 and 150%, with temperature columns spanning about -0.5 to -24 °C and missing cells where the
  apparatus could not reach a condition. All specimens grew in air on isolated c-axis needle tips.
- Each composite panel is a representative specimen selected from several growing crystals, with
  explicit preference for well-formed and symmetric examples. The 206 panels therefore form a
  curated morphology atlas, not 206 random samples or replicate-rate estimates. Preserve each
  panel's campaign/condition identity and do not infer population frequencies.
- Stated temperature uncertainty is typically +/-0.2 °C and actual supersaturation is estimated
  between 0.8 and 1.2 times the label. The two-dimensional projections can obscure three-dimensional
  structure. These source uncertainties are materially larger than panel-reading precision and must
  remain separate from any future image-coding uncertainty.
- The matrix visibly includes warm plates, the -5 to -7 °C needle/column regime, plates across
  -11 to -16 °C and colder needles/rosettes at low supersaturation. Turning this into a Phase 9
  target requires a predeclared morphology codebook plus specimen-level split policy; it does not
  require inventing numeric sizes from the displayed fields of view.

This is the largest condition-labelled direct image set in the four-source batch. It is highly
relevant to Phase 9 behavioral scoring, but its c-axis needle seed, curated sampling, broad
condition uncertainty and in-sample relationship to M1 forbid treating it as a simple plate-seed
held-out validation set.

## Batch coverage and handoff

- Four sources / 76 pages inspected visually, page-completely.
- Direct measured or observational content: all four sources, with the largest units being the
  Fig. 8 controlled run in `2106.09809v1`, the Fig. 24 qualitative matrix in `2109.00098v1`, the
  mixed archived measurements in Figs. 4--9 of `2306.04042v1`, and the 206-panel matrix in
  `2306.13087v1`.
- The principal duplicate/lineage hazards are prior-study figures reprinted in the two 2021 papers,
  multiple views of the same specimen, and the likely overlap between the two taxonomy campaigns.
- Formal S3 must assign stable measurement-unit identifiers, reconcile specimen/campaign lineage,
  and decide which plots/images merit transcription or digitization. This record does not make
  those operator choices.
